# Energy futures trend and carry

## In plain words

Our commodity trend test failed, but it used gas prices nobody could trade. We rebuilt the data from real futures contracts for oil, heating oil, gasoline and gas, rolled the way a fund would, and test trend and carry on them. The free prices end in April 2024, so the test does too.

## Question

On rolled NYMEX energy futures from 1999-01-04 to 2024-04-05, do 12-month trend and cross-sectional carry each pass at least four of the five reality tests, after 3 bp per unit traded and 2 bp per roll? Registered as [REG-0007](../../registrations/REG-0007-energy-futures-trend-and-carry.json).

## Setup

- Prices: EIA copies of NYMEX settlements for contracts 1 to 3 of WTI crude (from 1983), heating oil (contract 2 from 1994), natural gas (1994) and RBOB gasoline (October 2005), all ending 5 April 2024.
- Returns: hold contract 2; when it becomes contract 1 (found from each contract's expiry rule and confirmed from prices within three days), roll into the new contract 2 at that close, paying 2 bp. This never holds a contract in its last month, so the negative WTI settlement of 20 April 2020 is not in the index.
- `energy_tsmom`: sign of the 12-month return, inverse 60-day volatility weights, monthly. `energy_carry`: sign of 12 * ln(C2 / C3) minus the cross-sectional median, inverse volatility weights, monthly.
- No strategy return was computed before registering; only roll days and data coverage were checked.

## Threats to validity

- **Four contracts.** All four are energy and move together, so neither strategy is diversified like the 20 to 58 contracts in the papers.
- **Natural gas seasonality.** The gas curve slopes up and down with the seasons, so part of the carry signal is a calendar effect.
- **Roll day errors.** Where the curve is flat, the roll day may be off by a day, which changes that day's return by about the spread between contracts.
- **Data ends in April 2024.** No forward test is possible with this source.
