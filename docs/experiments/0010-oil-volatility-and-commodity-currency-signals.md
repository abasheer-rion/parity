# Oil volatility and commodity currency signals

## In plain words

Two new ideas on oil. First, selling insurance against big oil price swings, which research says pays most of the time and loses badly in crashes. Second, using the currencies of commodity-exporting countries to predict energy prices, which turns our failed oil idea around. Both run from April 2008 to April 2024.

## Question

Do a short crude oil variance swap (before swap costs) and a commodity currency signal for energy futures each pass at least four of the five reality tests from 2008-04-01 to 2024-04-05? Registered as [REG-0008](../../registrations/REG-0008-oil-volatility-and-commodity-currencies.json).

## Setup

- `oil_variance_short`: each month, a short 30-day variance swap struck at OVX squared at the last close of the previous month; marked daily with realised variance of the rolled WTI index so far and OVX for the rest of the month; one volatility point at the strike costs about 0.2% of capital. No swap trading costs.
- `commodity_fx_leads_energy`: average 3-month log change of AUD, CAD, NZD (FRED) and ZAR (Fed H.10) against USD; long all energy futures when positive, short when negative, inverse volatility weights, monthly; 3 bp per unit traded and 2 bp per roll.
- No strategy return was computed before registering; only coverage, roll days and that the variance index stays above zero were checked.

## Threats to validity

- **Variance swap costs are missing.** Real swaps or option strips cost several volatility points; the result is an upper bound.
- **OVX is built from options on USO**, which held near-month futures, while our realised variance uses a contract 2 index. The two are close but not the same.
- **The commodity currency signal moves with the dollar**, so it may be a disguised dollar or risk-appetite bet.
- **The two ideas share one registration** only because they share the dates; each is judged on its own.
