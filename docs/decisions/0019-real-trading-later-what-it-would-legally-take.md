# Real trading later, what it would legally take

## In plain words

The owner wants Parity to be "free to eventually trade". Today Parity trades nothing: Practice mode uses pretend money in the browser ([DEC-0018](0018-markets-on-the-product-side-crypto-perps-etfs-and-what-is-new.md)). This page lists what real trading would need, so the owner can decide later. **This is a future decision for the owner, not a plan we have started.** It is a summary for planning, not legal advice; a securities lawyer must check it before anything is built.

## Context

[INT-0002](../intent/0002-two-products-a-research-and-simulation-platform-and-a-public-trading-app.md) rules out trading real money for users, and advice to individuals, before the legal and regulatory work is done. "Free" trading apps in the US earn money in other ways, such as payment for order flow, interest on cash, margin lending and crypto spreads, and each of those brings its own rules and disclosures.

## Decision

Proposed, for the owner to accept or reject later. Nothing below is built.

1. **Do not become a broker ourselves.** Registering as a broker-dealer means SEC registration, FINRA membership, net capital, audits and staff licences. The usual route for a small app is a partner:
   - a broker-dealer that offers trading through an API and holds the accounts and money, for example Alpaca or DriveWealth;
   - or an introducing-broker arrangement, where a licensed firm carries the accounts and we bring the customers.
   Either way the partner's agreement decides what we may say, show and charge.
2. **Crypto is a separate licence problem.** Holding or exchanging crypto for customers can need state money transmitter licences, New York's BitLicense, and FinCEN registration. The simple route is again a licensed partner that holds the coins. Perps are derivatives; US customers can only trade them on CFTC-registered venues, so offshore perps stay out.
3. **Know your customer.** Opening real accounts needs identity checks, anti-money-laundering screening and sanctions checks. Partners usually run these, but we would store personal data and need a privacy policy and security to match.
4. **No personal advice.** Parity must not tell any one person what to buy. Personalised recommendations would bring investment adviser registration and suitability rules. We keep the product's current rules: show evidence and risks, never "buy", never rank by return. If an AI helper ever suggests trades, that is a new decision with legal review.
5. **Before any real money:** a lawyer's review, the partner contract, disclosures and terms of service, complaint handling, records kept as the rules require, and a check that the research side stays walled off ([DEC-0015](0015-two-sides-a-product-app-and-a-research-app-kept-apart.md)).

## Consequences

- Until the owner accepts this and the legal work is done, Practice stays pretend and nothing on the site takes money or places orders.
- When the owner wants to move, the first step is talking to one or two broker partners and a securities lawyer, not writing code.
- Costs are unknown today: partner fees, legal fees, licences and compliance staff. They should be priced before deciding.
