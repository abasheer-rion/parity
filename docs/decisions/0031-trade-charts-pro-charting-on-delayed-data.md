# Trade charts, pro charting on delayed data

## In plain words

The product app has a new **Trade** area (`/app/#/trade`) with the kind of chart trading apps use: candles, OHLC bars, line, area, Heikin-Ashi and baseline charts; daily, weekly and monthly bars; indicators such as moving averages, Bollinger bands, RSI, MACD, Stochastic, ATR, ADX, OBV, VWAP and volume, each with its own settings; drawing tools (horizontal line, trend line, Fibonacci) saved to your account; a crosshair legend, a compare overlay, log scale and fullscreen.

All prices are end of day and delayed. Every chart says where its data comes from, how old it is and how its bars were made. Nothing here places an order; real trading stays a future decision for the owner ([DEC-0019](0019-real-trading-later-what-it-would-legally-take.md)).

## Context

The owner asked for "an actual trading platform" that people and researchers can use to test ideas without real money. The first part is charting. We already export every series we may show for the Parity Terminal ([DEC-0024](0024-parity-terminal-a-market-terminal-of-its-own-at-the-sim-address.md)): FX, rates, commodities, US equity indices and crypto, each with its source and licence (REF-0014, REF-0015). Those sources give one closing price a day. None of them gives intraday prices or the day's high and low, and no free source lets us show single stocks.

## Decision

1. **The chart library is TradingView Lightweight Charts** (Apache 2.0), wrapped as one reusable part, `ProChart` in `web/src/ui/proChart.tsx`. Its licence asks for a link to tradingview.com; the chart shows the TradingView logo and a "Charts by TradingView" line under every chart.
2. **Indicators are computed in our own code** (`web/src/lib/indicators.ts`) with unit tests, including the RSI worked example from StockCharts. RSI, ATR and ADX use Wilder's smoothing, Bollinger uses the population standard deviation, Stochastic is the slow version. VWAP, OBV and volume appear only where the source gives volume (CoinGecko's daily volume for coins, now in the Markets export); we never invent volume.
3. **Bars are built from closes and say so.** A daily bar opens at the previous close, and its high and low are the larger and smaller of the two closes. Weekly and monthly bars are the first, highest, lowest and last daily close of the period. Daily charts start in 2016 because the export keeps one close a week before that. Intraday timeframes are not offered.
4. **Every chart is labelled** with its source, licence, the date of the last value, how many days old it is, and "Delayed, not live".
5. **Drawings are saved per person** in a new Supabase table, `trade_drawings`, with row level security so each person reads and writes only their own rows. The local preview without sign-in keeps them in the browser instead.
6. **No layout shift.** The chart's height is fixed from its settings before the first paint, the loading state has the same toolbar and size, and the page passes `npm run cls` at 1440 and 390 pixels, on the fast and the slow network.

## Consequences

- Trackers, a screener and a demo account with paper trading build on the same chart and data layer in later changes.
- A real intraday or stock feed would need a paid licence with display rights; until then the Trade area stays end of day.
- Candles built from closes understate how far prices moved within a day, so wick-based patterns (REF-0012) cannot be read from our daily candles. The Academy lesson "Reading candlesticks" says this.
