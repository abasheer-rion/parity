# Open sign-up with Supabase Auth replaces the Cloudflare login

## In plain words

Anyone can now make a Parity account and go straight into the app. There is a simple public front page, a sign-up page and a log-in page. Behind them, a small check on Cloudflare only hands out the app and the research data to people who are signed in. The old Cloudflare login, which only let in a fixed list of team emails, goes away once this is live.

## Context

The site sat behind Cloudflare Access: a page that emailed a code to a fixed list of team addresses. The owner asked (2026-10-21) to drop that gate, let anyone sign up, add a very simple public front page, and send people straight into the app with no onboarding. The owner also asked for Google and GitHub sign-in, passkeys and authenticator apps.

We looked at three options:

| Option | Cost now | Anyone can sign up | Extra service |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Keep only the Cloudflare login | Free | No: a gate for known emails | No |
| Supabase Auth | Free (50,000 monthly users on the free plan) | Yes | No, already our database |
| A separate login provider | Free at first, paid later | Yes | Yes |

Supabase Auth wins: we already use Supabase for the database, its row level security already reads the signed-in user, and it supports email and password, email codes, Google and GitHub, authenticator apps, and (as an experiment) passkeys.

## Decision

1. The site has two parts. Public: the front page (`/`, plain HTML), `/login/`, `/signup/`, `/reset/`, `/verify/`, and the brand files. Private: the app (`/app/`) and the research data (`/data/`).
2. A Cloudflare Pages function checks the Supabase session cookie on every private request. It verifies the token's signature with Supabase's published public keys, so it needs no secret. No session: `/app/` sends you to `/login/`, `/data/` answers 401.
3. Anyone can sign up. Any signed-in person can read published research. The team list only gives a badge and is no longer copied into the site.
4. Email confirmation is off for now, because Supabase's own sender cannot email people outside the team. All emails are designed and ready ([auth/README.md](../../auth/README.md)); confirmation comes back on once an email provider is connected.
5. Everything about sign-in and email lives in the top-level `auth/` folder. The browser side lives in `web/src/auth/`, the check in `web/functions/`.
6. Google, GitHub, Apple and Microsoft buttons show only for providers listed in a GitHub variable. Authenticator apps are on. Passkeys are built but hidden until switched on, because Supabase marks them experimental.
7. Settings keep two sections: Account and Appearance. The other sections are hidden, not deleted.

## Consequences

- Strangers can now read every published result and doc. That is the point, but anything private must not be put in `/data/` or the docs.
- With confirmation off, an email address on an account is not proof of who someone is. Nothing may grant access based on the email alone; the team badge is cosmetic.
- The middleware checks that a session exists, not that the second step was done. The app asks for the second step before it shows anything, but the files themselves only need a session.
- Some steps need the owner: push the auth settings with an access token, switch off Cloudflare Access, and optionally create the Google and GitHub sign-in apps ([auth/README.md](../../auth/README.md), [auth/PROVIDERS.md](../../auth/PROVIDERS.md)).
