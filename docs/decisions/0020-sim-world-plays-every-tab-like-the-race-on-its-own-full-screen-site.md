# Sim World plays every tab like the race, on its own full-screen site

## In plain words

Every Sim World tab now plays the same way as the strategy race. Each one has:

- a big date;
- a Play button and a speed of 1x, 4x or 16x;
- event bands on the timeline that you can click to replay;
- a leaderboard that re-ranks as time moves, with the leader highlighted and named under the date.

There are ten tabs in two groups:

- **Play:** Map, Crises, Commodities, Rates, Stocks and Connections.
- **Analyse:** Scenario, Paths, Stress test and the Strategy race.

Sim World also has its own site, `sim.endyland.com`, or `/sim/` on previews. It shows only the simulator and one Back button. The research side opens it in a new tab.

Everything still replays or reshuffles history. Nothing in it is a forecast. The owner asked for "a better section for world simming", playing "the same exact type of sequence", with more tabs, on a "brand new website sub domain".

## Context

[DEC-0016](0016-sim-world-2-a-world-map-scenarios-on-it-and-simulated-paths.md) added the map, scenarios and paths, each with its own controls. Only the strategy race had the full replay: the date, the speed, the bands and the moving leaderboard. People liked that sequence and wanted it everywhere.

## Decision

1. **One playback for every tab.** It lives in `web/src/pages/sim/play/` and has three parts:
   - a clock that plays, steps with the arrow keys, and jumps to the end when someone prefers reduced motion;
   - a stage with the date, the controls, the visual, the leaderboard and the timeline;
   - a leaderboard with three columns.

   Most tabs are one shared race of weekly series with an optional picture on top.
2. **The tabs.**
   - **Map:** currencies ranked by their move since the start of the window. Clicking a row lights up that currency's countries.
   - **Crises:** seven windows: dot-com, 2008, the euro crisis, the taper tantrum, the 2014 to 2016 oil crash, Covid and 2022. Each window starts a little before the stress and ends a little after.
   - **Commodities:** oil (WTI and Brent), gas and copper, with a producer map. Gas is labelled as the spot price. Copper is labelled as a monthly average.
   - **Rates:** the Treasury curve (3 months, 2, 10 and 30 years) changes shape over time. Rate gaps by currency race beside it, along with the 10 year minus 3 month slope.
   - **Stocks:** the 12 US industries and the research factors.
   - **Connections:** a heat map of rolling correlations between nine major assets.
   - **Scenario:** plays from day 0 to day 63 after the event. The map and the fan charts fill in day by day.
   - **Paths:** the simulated paths draw in week by week, and the risk table follows the week shown.
   - **Stress test:** a mix you choose, bought at the start of each crisis window and left alone, with a table of every crisis.
3. **More series in the world file.** `parity world export` now also writes the 12 industries, 7 factors, the Treasury yields and the VIX, all weekly.
   - Yields and the VIX are marked as levels, so the page never treats them as returns.
   - Copper is dated a week after each month ends, when it could first be read, as the backtests do.
   - The week still in progress carries the date of its last close.
   - The file is about 530 kB compressed.
4. **The full-screen site.**
   - `web/sim/index.html` is a third entry next to `/app/` and `/research/`. It keeps the sign-in check and uses hash routes such as `#/crises`.
   - Back closes the tab when Parity opened it. Otherwise it goes to the research home.
   - `simUrl()` in `web/src/lib/space.ts` points to `sim.endyland.com` on endyland hosts and to `/sim/` everywhere else.
   - On the research side, every link to Sim World opens a new tab. `#/sim` stays as a small card, so old links still work.
   - The host rewrite, `/sim/` protection and the shared sign-in cookie are server work, handled separately.

## Consequences

- With ten tabs, the tab bar wraps into its two groups and never scrolls sideways, even at phone width.
- The industry and factor data comes from Kenneth French's library, which runs about two months behind. The Stocks race therefore ends earlier than the currency tabs.
- Rate gaps come from monthly OECD rates, carried forward. Only nine currencies besides the dollar have one.
- Links inside Sim World that point to research pages, such as docs and strategies, open on the research side in a new tab.
- Engine tests cover the new export fields (`engine/tests/test_world.py`). Backtest results do not change.
