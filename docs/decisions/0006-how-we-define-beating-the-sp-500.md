# What "beating the S&P 500" means for us

## In plain words

Beating the stock market is easy to fake: take bigger risks, or try a hundred ideas and show the lucky one. So we set strict rules before looking at results. A strategy only counts as better if it wins fairly, at the same risk, after costs, and not by luck.

## Context

A currency portfolio and a stock index have very different risk. Raw returns are not a fair comparison: a currency book at 4% volatility will almost always earn less than stocks at 18%, and almost any strategy can be levered to show a higher return. We also need to protect ourselves from finding something that only looks good because we tried many variants.

## Decision

A strategy beats the S&P 500 only if all of these hold over the same dates, on data it was not tuned on:

1. **Higher risk-adjusted return.** Sharpe ratio above SPY's (dividends reinvested).
2. **Higher return at the same risk.** When scaled to SPY's long-run volatility (15% a year by default), its annual return (CAGR) is above SPY's.
3. **Not just luck.** Deflated Sharpe ratio of at least 0.95, counting every strategy variant we have run.
4. **Survives costs.** Still passes 1 to 3 at twice our default cost assumption, and the break-even cost is comfortably above realistic spreads.
5. **Holds out of sample.** Passes on a held-back period that was not used to choose parameters.

We also report, but do not require, low correlation with SPY. A strategy with a lower Sharpe than SPY that is uncorrelated with it can still improve a portfolio, and we will say so when we see it.

Definitions of each number are in [REF-0002](../reference/0002-kpis.md).

## Consequences

- The dashboard always shows SPY alongside every strategy, at both raw and matched risk.
- Most results will be "no". That is expected and is still a result.
