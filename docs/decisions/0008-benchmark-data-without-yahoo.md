# Measure the benchmark with Fama and French and FRED, not Yahoo

## In plain words

We were getting stock market prices from Yahoo, but Yahoo's rules do not allow programs to download its data. So we switched to two sources that do allow it: a famous free data set run by two finance professors, and the US central bank's data website. Together they track the S&P 500 almost exactly, and they go back much further.

## Context

Our benchmark was SPY from Yahoo's chart endpoint. Yahoo's terms forbid automated collection without permission ([DAT-0036](../datasets/0036-yahoo-chart.md)), so any result built on it sits in a grey area. Free alternatives each have a gap: FRED's S&P 500 series has only ten years and no dividends; Stooq blocks scripts; the Kenneth French library has total returns back to 1926 but publishes about two months late.

## Decision

- The benchmark is `eq.US_TOTAL`: the Fama and French US market total return (market excess return plus the T-bill rate, all US stocks, dividends included), extended past French's last date with FRED's S&P 500 daily price returns plus a dividend accrual equal to the gap between the two over the previous year.
- The benchmark strategy is now `us_market_buy_hold`. Every report and the gauntlet compare against it.
- SPY from Yahoo stays only as an optional cross-check that never feeds a result. Official runs do not fail if Yahoo is down.
- Every report automatically checks sources against each other (benchmark vs SPY, French vs the S&P 500 index, WTI vs Brent) and shows the correlation and return gap on the Data health page.
- Backtests now start on 1999-01-04, the euro's first trading day.

## Consequences

- The benchmark is the whole US market, not the S&P 500 exactly. Since 1999 it returned 8.8% a year against 8.4% for SPY, with a daily correlation of about 0.98. Beating it is slightly harder than beating SPY.
- The last one or two months of the benchmark are an approximation until French publishes. They are replaced automatically on the next monthly update, which changes the data fingerprint, as it should.
- RUN-000001 and RUN-000002 used SPY from Yahoo. They stay in the ledger as recorded.
