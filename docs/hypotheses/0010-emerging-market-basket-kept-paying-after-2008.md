# Emerging market basket kept paying after 2008

## In plain words

A well-known study found that simply owning 20 emerging market currencies against the dollar paid as much as stocks with a third of the risk, from 1999 to 2008. Its data stopped a month before the 2008 crash. We test whether the same simple basket kept paying afterwards.

## Claim

From February 2010, an equal-weighted long basket of every emerging market currency in our data against the dollar, rebalanced monthly, has a Sharpe ratio reliably above zero after 10 basis points of cost per unit traded, and passes at least four of the five reality tests in [REF-0006](../reference/0006-the-gauntlet.md).

## Why it might be true

- Gilmore and Hayashi (2011) report 6.6% a year at 4.9% volatility (Sharpe 1.34) for 1999 to 2008 ([RES-0011](../research/0011-emerging-market-currencies.md)).
- The basket earns the average EM interest premium, which stayed large.

## Why it might be false

- Their sample was an EM boom with a falling dollar; the dollar rose for much of 2011 to 2022.
- The authors had a commercial interest and proprietary data.
- A long-only basket is a bet against the dollar and on global growth, which tends to lose when stocks fall.

## How we test it

1. Pre-registered in `REG-0006` with the other EM strategies, same start, costs and pass bar.
2. The question is only whether the post-2008 basket earned anything, not whether it beat stocks.
3. Results in [EXP-0007](../experiments/0007-emerging-market-currencies-since-2010.md).

## Result so far

None yet. Registered on 2026-10-21; no performance number has been computed for this rule by anyone on the team.
