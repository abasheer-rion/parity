# The crack spread mean-reverts after costs

## In plain words

A refinery turns three barrels of crude oil into roughly two barrels of gasoline and one of heating oil. The difference between what those products sell for and what the crude costs is the refining margin, called the crack spread. Refiners run harder when it is wide and slow down when it is narrow, so it should drift back to normal. We test a plain version: when the margin is far from its average of the past year, bet that it returns, and stop when it gets back to the average. Unlike all our other energy strategies, this one is not a bet on the price of oil.

## Claim

From 2006-10-02 to 2024-04-05, `crack_spread_reversion` on rolled NYMEX WTI crude oil, RBOB gasoline and heating oil futures has a Sharpe ratio reliably above zero after 3 basis points of cost per unit traded and 2 basis points per roll, and passes at least four of the five reality tests in [REF-0006](../reference/0006-the-gauntlet.md).

The rule, every week:

1. **The spread:** (2 x gasoline + 1 x heating oil - 3 x crude) / 3, in dollars per barrel, from contract 2 prices (gasoline and heating oil times 42 gallons).
2. **How unusual it is:** its z-score against the past 252 trading days (mean and standard deviation over the full window, nothing shorter).
3. **Entry:** z below -2, buy the spread (long gasoline and heating oil, short crude); z above +2, sell it.
4. **Exit:** close when z crosses 0. Between the thresholds and the mean, a position is kept and no new one is opened. A missing price closes the position.
5. **Sizing:** each leg's weight is its dollar value in one crack spread (2, 1 and 3 barrels), scaled to a gross exposure of 1, using the rolled indices `fut.RB`, `fut.HO` and `fut.CL`. Weights are set on the last trading day of each week.

## Why it might be true

- Crude, gasoline and heating oil prices are tied together by refining, so their spread should mean-revert. Karathanasopoulos, Dunis and Khalil (2016) forecast the 3:2:1 spread on 2005 to 2015 data and report a net annualised return of 42.74% for their best model, after costs of about 10% a year ([ALG-0099](../algorithms/0099-crack-spread-mean-reversion.md)).
- The spread is close to market-neutral for oil: a parallel move in all three prices cancels out. That makes it unlike our other energy strategies, which are mostly bets on oil ([RES-0019](../research/0019-trading-the-energy-strategies-costs-contracts-and-data-after-april-2024.md)).
- The rule trades only when the spread is two standard deviations from normal, so it trades rarely and costs stay small.

## Why it might be false

- **The published evidence is weak for our rule.** The paper's best model is chosen from six neural networks with 250 inputs, and its stated 2,087 trading days do not match its dates. Our simple z-score rule is not what they tested.
- **Seasons.** Gasoline margins are high in summer and low in winter. A one-year window mixes seasons, so part of a "wide" reading in June is just summer.
- **Regime shifts.** Refinery closures, hurricanes (2005, 2008, 2017) and the 2022 diesel squeeze moved the spread for months. A rule that sells a wide spread keeps losing while it widens.
- **Gasoline grade change.** The EIA's RBOB series starts in October 2005, after the switch from unleaded, so the window starts a year later than any earlier gasoline data.
- **Ratio, not refinery yield.** Real refineries do not produce exactly 2:1; the 3:2:1 is a market convention.
- **The paper's sample overlaps ours** (2005 to 2015), so this is not an out-of-sample test of their result. It is a test of the simple rule.

## How we test it

1. Pre-registered in `REG-0010`. Window (252 days), entry (2 standard deviations), exit (the mean) and weekly rebalancing are fixed. The gauntlet's sensitivity test tries windows of 126 and 504 days.
2. Pass bar: at least 4 of 5 reality tests.
3. `prior_trials` counts every earlier registration (316). No variant of this rule has been run.
4. The official run ends on 5 April 2024, when the EIA stopped publishing the prices.

## Result so far

None yet. Registered on 2026-10-25. Before registering, only unit tests on made-up prices were run. No return, Sharpe ratio or other performance number has been computed for this rule, or for the crack spread itself on real prices, by anyone on the team.
