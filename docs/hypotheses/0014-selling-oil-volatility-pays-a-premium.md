# Selling oil volatility pays a premium

## In plain words

People pay to protect themselves from big oil price swings, and on average they pay more than the swings cost. Selling them that protection should earn money most months and lose a lot in a few. We test how much, before trading costs we cannot measure, so the answer is a best case.

## Claim

From 2008-04-01 to 2024-04-05, selling a 30-day crude oil variance swap each month at OVX squared, marked daily with realised variance from rolled WTI futures, has a Sharpe ratio reliably above zero and passes at least four of the five reality tests in [REF-0006](../reference/0006-the-gauntlet.md), before variance swap trading costs.

## Why it might be true

- Shorting crude oil variance had a Sharpe ratio of 0.59 in 1996 to 2006 (Trolle and Schwartz, 2010), and variance buyers lost in 18 of 21 commodity markets (Prokopczuk et al., 2017).
- The S&P 500 version of the same trade is one of the best documented premia.

## Why it might be false

- It loses most in crashes: late 2008 and March and April 2020 are in the window.
- OVX is built from options on USO, an ETF that held near-month futures, so it is not exactly a swap on our rolled index.
- Real variance swaps cost several volatility points to trade, which could remove the premium.

## How we test it

1. Pre-registered in `REG-0008`. Starts 2008-04-01 with the commodity currency test; OVX starts in May 2007.
2. Pass bar: at least 4 of 5 reality tests. Test B5 (does not crash with stocks) is expected to fail and matters most for [RES-0014](../research/0014-famous-currency-and-commodity-traders-and-what-their-records-teach.md)'s warning.
3. Results in [EXP-0009](../experiments/0009-oil-volatility-and-commodity-currency-signals.md).

## Result so far

None yet. Registered on 2026-10-21; no performance number has been computed for this rule by anyone on the team.
