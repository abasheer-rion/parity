# Macro and energy surprises move currencies in a predictable direction

## In plain words

When important news comes out, like how many people got jobs, money markets move. The idea: if the news is a big surprise, the price keeps moving the same way for a while, long enough for us to catch some of it.

## Claim

When a scheduled release (US jobs, CPI, central bank decisions, the weekly EIA oil and gas inventory reports) comes in far from expectations, the affected currency keeps moving in the direction of the surprise for minutes to days, long enough for a strategy using free data to capture part of it.

## Why it might be true

- Macro surprises move the dollar quickly and in the direction theory predicts (Andersen, Bollerslev, Diebold and Vega, 2003).
- Much of the effect of news arrives through trading flows over hours, not one instant jump (Evans and Lyons, 2008).

## Why it might be false

- Most of the move happens within seconds to minutes, before free data or a home connection can react ([DEC-0005](../decisions/0005-no-microsecond-trading-in-the-poc.md)).
- A 2026 meta-analysis of 31 event studies finds the average news effect on exchange rates is close to zero once publication bias is corrected (Bortnikova, 2026; see [the reading list](../research/0001-reading-list.md#macro-news-and-announcements)).
- Getting consensus forecasts for free, with the values known before the release, is hard.

## How we test it

Not started. First steps: find a free, point-in-time source of release values and consensus forecasts, then measure the currency move after large surprises at daily resolution before attempting anything intraday.

## Result so far

None yet.
