# The S&P 500 plus an FX strategy beats the S&P 500 alone

## Claim

Holding SPY together with a currency strategy, at the same total volatility as SPY alone, gives a higher Sharpe ratio and smaller drawdowns than SPY alone.

## Why it might be true

Kroencke, Schindler and Schrimpf (2014) found adding FX styles to a global stock and bond portfolio raised the out-of-sample Sharpe ratio from 0.64 to 1.05. Barroso and Santa-Clara (2015) found something similar. Our own baseline shows carry and trend have low or negative correlation with SPY ([EXP-0001](../experiments/0001-baseline-backtests.md)).

## Why it might be false

The diversification gain can vanish in exactly the crises when it is needed, since carry tends to crash with stocks.

## How we test it

Add a combined portfolio to the report: SPY plus each FX strategy at equal risk, scaled to SPY's volatility. This does not replace the stricter test in [DEC-0006](../decisions/0006-how-we-define-beating-the-sp-500.md); we report both.
