# Energy futures trend pays on tradable prices

## In plain words

Our commodity trend test lost almost everything, but it used gas prices nobody could actually trade. We rerun the same idea on real futures contracts for oil, heating oil, gasoline and gas, rolled the way a fund would roll them, to get a fair answer.

## Claim

From 1999-01-04 to 2024-04-05 (when the free futures prices end), a 12-month time series momentum rule on rolled NYMEX crude oil, heating oil, gasoline and natural gas futures, with inverse volatility weights and monthly rebalancing, has a Sharpe ratio reliably above zero after 3 basis points of cost per unit traded and 2 basis points per roll, and passes at least four of the five reality tests in [REF-0006](../reference/0006-the-gauntlet.md).

## Why it might be true

- Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen (2012) find 12-month momentum positive in every one of 58 futures markets, 1985 to 2009; AQR's long backtest reports a net Sharpe ratio of 0.62 for 2000 to 2013 ([RES-0014](../research/0014-commodity-futures-carry-trend-and-volatility.md)).
- Trend's crisis gains (2008, 2022 in the industry indices) are what made our currency trend useful.

## Why it might be false

- Four energy contracts are highly correlated, so this is close to a single bet on oil.
- Trend funds had a poor decade after 2009.
- The earlier spot-price version is already a seen trial on the same idea.

## How we test it

1. Pre-registered in `REG-0007`. Ends on 2024-04-05 because the EIA stopped publishing futures prices then.
2. Pass bar: at least 4 of 5 reality tests.
3. Counts the earlier `commodity_tsmom` runs as prior trials.
4. Results in [EXP-0009](../experiments/0009-energy-futures-trend-and-carry.md).

## Result so far

None yet. Registered on 2026-10-21; no performance number has been computed for this rule by anyone on the team.
