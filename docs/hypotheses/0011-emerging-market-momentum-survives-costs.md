# Emerging market momentum survives costs

## In plain words

Among the big currencies, buying the recent winners and selling the recent losers did not work for us. Researchers say this kind of momentum lives in smaller currencies, where fewer traders compete. We test it on emerging market currencies, after costs.

## Claim

From February 2010, a strategy long the third of emerging market currencies with the highest past 3-month excess return and short the lowest third, rebalanced monthly, has a Sharpe ratio reliably above zero after 10 basis points of cost per unit traded, and passes at least four of the five reality tests in [REF-0006](../reference/0006-the-gauntlet.md).

## Why it might be true

- Menkhoff et al. (2012) find currency momentum up to about 10% a year before costs and about 4% after for the best version, concentrated in minor currencies; among developed currencies it is "basically non-existent after transaction costs".
- Our G10 cross-sectional momentum failed ([EXP-0004](../experiments/0004-all-strategies-1999.md)), which fits that account.

## Why it might be false

- The profit lives where costs are highest; 10 bp may still be too low for some EM currencies.
- Short positions in high-rate EM currencies pay their carry, which momentum may not cover.

## How we test it

1. Pre-registered in `REG-0006`: lookback 63 trading days, top and bottom thirds, monthly.
2. Same start, costs and pass bar as HYP-0009.
3. Results in [EXP-0007](../experiments/0007-emerging-market-currencies-since-2010.md).

## Result so far

None yet. Registered on 2026-10-21; no performance number has been computed for this rule by anyone on the team.
