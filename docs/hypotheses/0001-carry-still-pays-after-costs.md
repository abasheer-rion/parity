# Currency carry still pays after costs

## In plain words

Some countries pay more interest on their money than others. The idea: borrow money where interest is low, put it where interest is high, and keep the difference. Textbooks say this should not work. In real life it has worked on average, but it sometimes loses a lot very fast.

## Claim

Holding the three highest-rate G10 currencies against the three lowest, rebalanced monthly, earns a positive return after realistic costs, and its Sharpe ratio is competitive with the S&P 500 on a risk-matched basis.

Code: `engine/src/parity/strategies/fx_carry.py` (strategy id `fx_carry`).

## Why it might be true

- Uncovered interest parity fails in the data: high-rate currencies do not fall by as much as the rate gap (Fama, 1984).
- Carry returns look like payment for bearing global risk (Lustig, Roussanov and Verdelhan, 2011), and risk premia do not disappear just because people know about them.
- It trades rarely, so costs are small.

## Why it might be false

- It crashes in risk-off episodes (Brunnermeier, Nagel and Pedersen, 2008) and the rate gaps between G10 countries were tiny for most of 2009 to 2021.
- Published FX factors have decayed sharply after publication (Hutchinson et al., 2022).

See [the reading list](../research/0001-reading-list.md#foundations).

## How we test it

1. Backtest from 2000 with 1 bp costs, then 2 bp, with carry credited daily.
2. Compare Sharpe, CAGR at 15% volatility, skew and worst drawdown against SPY.
3. Split the sample at 2008 and 2020 to see if the effect has decayed.
4. Pass or fail against [DEC-0006](../decisions/0006-how-we-define-beating-the-sp-500.md).

## Result so far

[EXP-0001](../experiments/0001-baseline-backtests.md): Sharpe 0.39 against 0.50 for SPY from 2000. At 15% target volatility, 4.9% a year against 8.0% for SPY. The signal does carry information (mean rank IC of 0.03 to 0.05 at all horizons), but not enough to beat stocks. Not yet split by sub-period.

[EXP-0002](../experiments/0002-baseline-gauntlet.md), the gauntlet: fails all six tests against the S&P 500, passes three of five reality tests. Verdict: probably a small, real risk premium that is consistent across eras but too small to beat stocks and that loses money when stocks crash. Status stays `testing` until we check our carry series against published academic portfolios.
