# Commodity currencies lead energy futures

## In plain words

We tried using oil prices to predict the Canadian dollar and it did not work. Research says the arrow points the other way: currencies of countries that export commodities move first, and commodity prices follow. So we flip the idea and use those currencies to decide whether to own energy futures.

## Claim

From 2008-04-01 (after the paper's sample) to 2024-04-05, holding all energy futures long when the average 3-month change of AUD, CAD, NZD and ZAR against the dollar is positive, and short when negative, inverse volatility weighted and rebalanced monthly, has a Sharpe ratio reliably above zero after 3 basis points of cost per unit traded and 2 basis points per roll, and passes at least four of the five reality tests in [REF-0006](../reference/0006-the-gauntlet.md).

## Why it might be true

- Chen, Rogoff and Rossi (2010) find commodity currency exchange rates forecast commodity prices out of sample, and the reverse is less robust.
- Ferraro, Rogoff and Rossi (2015) find the oil and Canadian dollar link mostly same-day, which explains why our oil-first idea failed ([EXP-0003](../experiments/0003-monthly-commodity-gap.md)).

## Why it might be false

- The paper forecasts broad commodity indices quarterly; we trade four energy contracts monthly.
- Better forecasts in a statistical sense need not make money after costs.
- Commodity currencies and oil both move with the dollar and global risk appetite, so the signal may be a disguised trend rule.

## How we test it

1. Pre-registered in `REG-0008`.
2. Pass bar: at least 4 of 5 reality tests.
3. Counts the earlier commodity gap trials (HYP-0003, HYP-0008) as prior trials on the commodity and currency link.
4. Results in [EXP-0010](../experiments/0010-oil-volatility-and-commodity-currency-signals.md).

## Result so far

None yet. Registered on 2026-10-21; no performance number has been computed for this rule by anyone on the team.
