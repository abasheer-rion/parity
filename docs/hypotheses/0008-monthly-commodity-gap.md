# A slower commodity gap works where the weekly one did not

## In plain words

Our oil idea failed when it traded every week. A version that traded once a month did better, and another measurement also hinted that the idea works slowly. But we only noticed this after looking at the results, which is exactly how people fool themselves. So we write it down as a new idea, and it has to pass every test from scratch, with a penalty for how many versions we already tried.

## Claim

The commodity gap signal for CAD and NOK, rebalanced monthly instead of weekly, has a Sharpe ratio reliably above zero after costs and passes at least four of the five reality tests in [REF-0006](../reference/0006-the-gauntlet.md).

## Why it might be true

- The signal's information coefficient rises with horizon, from 0.02 at one day to 0.125 at 63 days ([EXP-0001](../experiments/0001-baseline-backtests.md#results)).
- Kohlscheen, Avalos and Schrimpf (2017) found commodity prices predict commodity currencies for up to two months.
- In the gauntlet's sweep, all three monthly variants beat their weekly versions ([EXP-0002](../experiments/0002-baseline-gauntlet.md#commodity-gap)).

## Why it might be false

- It was found by looking at results. That alone makes it suspect.
- The best monthly Sharpe in the sweep was only 0.28, with two currencies and about 25 years of data.
- The oil to dollar link changed after 2020 (Rees, 2025).

## How we test it

1. Count at least 12 trials already spent on this signal (from EXP-0002) in the deflated Sharpe ratio.
2. Fix the settings before running: 20-day gap window, monthly rebalance, nothing else changed.
3. Run the full gauntlet, and report before and after 2020 separately.
4. If it passes, the only real confirmation is new data after 2026-10-20.

## Result so far

Rejected by its own pre-registered bar ([EXP-0003](../experiments/0003-monthly-commodity-gap.md)). It passed 2 of the 5 reality tests; the plan required 4.
