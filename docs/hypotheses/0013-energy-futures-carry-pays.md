# Energy futures carry pays

## In plain words

Some commodities cost more for delivery now than for delivery later, usually because stocks are low. Owning those and selling the opposite ones has paid well in research that covers 20 to 30 commodities, even after it was published. We test a small version with the four energy futures we can get for free.

## Claim

From 1999-01-04 to 2024-04-05, long the energy futures whose curve slope 12 * ln(C2 / C3) is above the cross-sectional median and short those below, inverse volatility weighted and rebalanced monthly, has a Sharpe ratio reliably above zero after 3 basis points of cost per unit traded and 2 basis points per roll, and passes at least four of the five reality tests in [REF-0006](../reference/0006-the-gauntlet.md).

## Why it might be true

- High-minus-low basis earned 9.2% a year (Sharpe 0.68) in 1959 to 2004 and 10.4% (Sharpe 1.04) out of sample in 2005 to 2014 (Bhardwaj, Gorton and Rouwenhorst, 2015).
- Koijen et al. (2018) find commodity carry Sharpe 0.60 on 24 commodities.

## Why it might be false

- Four contracts instead of 20 to 30, so far less diversification.
- Natural gas curves are seasonal, so its slope is partly a calendar effect, not scarcity.
- Carry strategies across asset classes lose together in global recessions.

## How we test it

1. Pre-registered in `REG-0007`, same dates, costs and pass bar as HYP-0012.
2. Default uses today's slope (smooth = 1), as the papers do.
3. Results in [EXP-0009](../experiments/0009-energy-futures-trend-and-carry.md).

## Result so far

None yet. Registered on 2026-10-21; no performance number has been computed for this rule by anyone on the team.
