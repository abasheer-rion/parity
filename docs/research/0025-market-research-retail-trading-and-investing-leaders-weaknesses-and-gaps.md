# Market research: retail trading and investing, leaders, weaknesses and gaps

## In plain words

We looked at who already sells trading and investing apps and research tools to ordinary people in the US, what they do badly, and what nobody offers yet. Big apps make money when people trade a lot, and their research is thin. Professionals pay tens of thousands of dollars a year to see how one piece of news spreads to other companies; ordinary investors get star ratings. That missing piece, a clear explanation of why news moves a stock, is the opening for Parity.

## Summary

Gathered on 24 and 25 October 2026 from pages we opened; every number links to its source, listed at the end. Company user counts are the companies' own claims. A shared working copy with a market map drawing lives at https://claude.ai/artifact/NcBntdPGZJAMaycnJyBFHr.

- **Leaders.** Fidelity ($19.9T) and Schwab ($13.33T) hold the assets. Robinhood (28.4M funded, $369B) sets the product agenda: options, prediction markets, tokenized stocks, private-company funds.
- **Weaknesses.** Robinhood and Webull rate 3/5 on research and depend on order flow (53.3% of Webull's 2025 revenue). Interactive Brokers overwhelms beginners. Seeking Alpha is crowd-written. Interface problems now drive more account closures than fees.
- **Gaps.** Supply-chain research costs $12K to $32K a seat per year; retail tools at $15 to $50 a month give ratings, not reasons. About 25% of US stocks have little or no analyst coverage. Schwab's May 2026 "what moved" tool covers only S&P 500 holdings and no knock-on effects. 30% of retail investors use AI to pick stocks, but only 7% say it drove their last big decision.
- **Private markets.** Schwab bought Forge and Morgan Stanley bought EquityZen; Linqto went bankrupt and faces an SEC fraud suit. Open-to-anyone access is listed funds (RVI, VCX, DXYZ) and APIs like Monark. We treat this as a later phase.
- **What we do not know.** No source measures whether retail investors understand why news moves a stock, which is Parity's core thesis.

## The industry

Parity would enter **US self-directed retail investing**: apps where individuals pick and trade their own investments, plus the research and strategy tools around them. It is crowded and growing fast in 2026. Four segments matter to us:

1. **Retail brokers**, the apps where money and trades live (Robinhood, Schwab, Fidelity, Webull).
2. **Strategy, research and AI tools** that help people decide what to trade (TradingView, Composer, AI assistants).
3. **Alternative assets for retail:** crypto, prediction markets, and private-company and private-fund access.
4. **Infrastructure** that lets new apps launch without becoming a broker (Alpaca, Apex, DriveWealth, Monark).

**How big it is**

- 62% of US adults owned stock in April 2025 ([Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/266807/percentage-americans-owns-stock.aspx)).
- Retail makes up roughly 30% of daily US stock volume, an estimate attributed to XTX Markets ([The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/the-trade-predictions-series-2026-retail-retail-retail/), January 2026).
- Schwab alone holds $13.33 trillion of client assets and Fidelity $19.9 trillion under administration, so the incumbents are enormous.
- Online trading platforms, as software, are put at $11.4 billion globally in 2025, growing about 5% a year to 2034 ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/online-trading-platform-market-statistics)). Treat analyst market-size figures as rough.

**What changed in 2026:** prediction markets went mainstream at Robinhood, Coinbase, Moomoo and Webull; the pattern day trader rule ended on 4 June 2026, lifting trading volumes; crypto revenue fell across the board; and brokers raced to add AI assistants and private-company exposure.

## Gaps in the market

The largest gap is between what professionals pay $12K to $32K a year for and what retail pays $15 to $50 a month for. Professionals get supply-chain data and explanations of how news spreads from one company to another. Retail gets ratings and lists of what moved. Retail investors already use AI for research, but few trust it enough to act on it.

## Openings for Parity

Parity's best opening is honest research that explains why a stock moves. That is the one gap nobody on the leader list fills: brokers earn more when you trade more, and the AI tools mostly speed up trading.

| Opening | Problem it answers | What Parity already has | What we'd need to win |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Explain the "why" behind news | Retail research comes from finfluencers. Bloomberg-level context costs about $30K a year. | The news-impact graph design (event to company to industry chains) | The graph built, a case library of past events, and a live track record |
| Strategies that show their failures | AI-washing cases; options losses; tools that only show winning backtests | The 11-test gauntlet and a published record of failed strategies | Run the gauntlet on stock strategies and publish every result, not only the winners |
| Research-first, not trading-first | Revenue tied to trading volume; PFOF; gamification fines | Nothing yet; this is a business-model choice | A subscription price, not order flow. Koyfin and Seeking Alpha show $10 to $80 a month is accepted |
| Private-company research | Linqto-style opaque pricing; the big platforms now belong to Schwab and Morgan Stanley | Nothing yet | Data from Caplight or Clarity, and research on the funds anyone can buy (RVI, VCX, DXYZ) |
| Private-market access in the app | Accreditation gates and $10K to $20K minimums | Nothing yet | A broker on Apex plus Monark, or wait for the INVEST Act. Plan this for later |

The main risk is that Robinhood, Public and Catalyst are all adding AI research inside the broker. Parity has to be clearly better at explaining *why*, not just another chat box. That makes the case library from the news-impact work the first thing to build: it is the proof.
