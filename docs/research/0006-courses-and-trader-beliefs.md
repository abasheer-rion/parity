# What trading courses and traders swear by, and what the evidence says

## In plain words

We looked at what trading courses and experienced traders tell beginners, and checked it against real numbers. Most people who trade on their own lose money: brokers say about seven in ten retail accounts lose, and long studies of day traders find almost none make a living. No course we found publishes honest results for its students. A few sayings hold up ("most traders fail", "costs matter", "do not over-tune your rules"); many popular ones have no solid evidence either way.

## Summary

Gathered 2026-10-20. Five CFD brokers checked that day disclosed that 69 to 77 percent of their retail accounts lose money. Among Brazilians who day traded for more than 300 days, 97 percent lost money. In Taiwan, fewer than 1 percent of day traders were reliably profitable. No course publishes audited student results; when the US Federal Trade Commission obtained Warrior Trading's own data, 74 percent of practice accounts had lost money. Pages that blocked our reader, or numbers seen only in summaries, are marked unverified. Six of the twelve beliefs in the final table can be tested with our engine.

## Short version

1. Most retail traders lose money, and the numbers are large and consistent. CFD brokers we checked today disclose that 69% to 77% of their retail accounts lose money. Among Brazilian day traders who kept going for more than 300 days, 97% lost money. In Taiwan, fewer than 1% of day traders were predictably profitable.
2. Paid discretionary day trading courses publish no student outcomes. The one case where a regulator looked at the data (the FTC and Warrior Trading) found that most customers lost money.
3. The common slogans are a mix. "Most traders fail" holds up strongly. "Trend is your friend" and "cut losses, let winners run" have real support in large studies, but the support is for slow, diversified, rules-based versions, and it has weakened in currencies since publication. "Risk 1 to 2 percent per trade", "journal everything" and "psychology first" have little direct large-sample evidence for or against.
4. Systematic practitioners tell newcomers to learn something else first: trading costs, overfitting, position sizing by volatility, and diversification across many instruments. Our engine can test most of these directly.

What stands out to us: the systematic sources sell tools and methods and make no profit promises. The discretionary day trading sources market "financial freedom", and when a regulator obtained data, most customers lost money. No course in either group publishes audited student trading results.

## Core beliefs and the evidence

### "Most traders fail"

Supported, strongly, and across countries and products.

- ESMA found that "74-89% of retail accounts typically lose money" on CFDs, with average losses per client of EUR 1,600 to 29,000 ([ESMA, 2018](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-agrees-prohibit-binary-options-and-restrict-cfds-protect-retail-investors)).
- Brazil: of 19,646 people who started day trading futures in 2013 to 2015, 1,551 kept going for more than 300 days. 97% of those lost money, only 1.1% earned more than the minimum wage, and the authors found "no evidence of learning" ([Chague, De-Losso and Giovannetti, 2020, SSRN 3423101](https://ebicapital.nl/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/day-trading.pdf)). The paper says this runs "contrary to what course providers claim".
- Taiwan, 1992 to 2006: "the vast majority of day traders are unprofitable, and many persist despite an extensive experience of losses". Fewer than 1% of day traders predictably earn profits, and more than 75% quit within two years ([Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean and Zhang, "Do Day Traders Rationally Learn About Their Ability?"](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/Day%20Traders/Day%20Trading%20and%20Learning%20110217.pdf)).
- One nuance: there is real skill at the top. The top 500 Taiwanese day traders from one year earned 28.1 basis points a day after fees the next year ([Barber, Lee, Liu and Odean, "The Cross-Section of Speculator Skill"](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)). So "most fail" is true, but "nobody can" is not.

### "Cut losses, let winners run"

Mixed, leaning supported for the general idea.

- For: ordinary investors do the opposite. Odean studied 10,000 accounts and found they prefer to sell winners and hold losers. Winners they sold beat losers they kept by 3.4 percentage points over the next year ([Odean, 1998, Journal of Finance](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers%20current%20versions/areinvestorsreluctant.pdf)).
- For: across 58 futures markets, returns persisted for 1 to 12 months. A rule that stays with recent winners and exits losers earned "substantial abnormal returns" ([Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen, 2012, JFE](https://w4.stern.nyu.edu/facdir/lpederse/papers/TimeSeriesMomentum.pdf)).
- Against or conditional: Kaminski and Lo's paper on stop-loss rules ([DOI, Journal of Financial Markets](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.finmar.2013.07.001)) reportedly finds stop-losses help only when prices trend and cost money under a random walk (unverified, we could not read the abstract). Persistence also partly reverses over longer horizons ([Moskowitz et al.](https://w4.stern.nyu.edu/facdir/lpederse/papers/TimeSeriesMomentum.pdf)).

### "The trend is your friend"

Mixed. It is well supported historically, but the edge has shrunk.

- For: time series momentum appears in each of 58 futures markets, currencies included ([Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen, 2012](https://w4.stern.nyu.edu/facdir/lpederse/papers/TimeSeriesMomentum.pdf)). Trend was positive in every decade since 1880 ([Hurst, Ooi and Pedersen, 2017](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2993026), via our earlier note). Two authors work at AQR, which sells trend funds.
- Against: after publication, FX carry, momentum and value went from an average Sharpe of +0.39 to -0.32 ([Hutchinson et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.irfa.2022.102245), via our earlier note). Carver reports his trend returns roughly halved after 2010 ([qoppac](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2025/10/is-degradation-of-trend-following.html), via our earlier note).
- What courses usually teach is different: short-term chart trends on one pair. The evidence above is for slow, diversified trend rules, not for intraday chart reading.

### "Risk 1 to 2 percent per trade"

Untestable as a universal rule. The idea behind it (keep each bet small enough to survive) is sound, but we found no large-sample study of the specific 1 to 2 percent figure.

- The theory for sizing bets comes from the Kelly criterion ([Kelly, 1956](https://www.princeton.edu/~wbialek/rome/refs/kelly_56.pdf), unverified, not read).
- Carver's book teaches sizing from an account-level risk target and each instrument's volatility, not a fixed percent per trade ([Leveraged Trading](https://www.systematicmoney.org/leveraged-trading); the details of his method are from our earlier notes and are unverified here).
- We can test it ourselves: run the same signals with fixed 1%, fixed 2% and volatility-targeted sizing and compare drawdowns.

### "The market is always right"

Untestable as stated. It is a reminder not to argue with prices. The nearest testable claim is that published edges shrink. That has support: equity anomaly returns are 58% lower after publication ([McLean and Pontiff, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12365), via our earlier note), and the FX results above point the same way. Technical FX profits also shrank over time ([Neely, Weller and Ulrich, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022109009090103), via our earlier note).

### "Psychology first" and "control your emotions"

Mixed and thin. In a study of 80 day traders, those with intense emotional reactions to gains and losses did significantly worse. The study found no distinct "trader personality" ([Lo, Repin and Steenbarger, 2005, NBER w11243](https://www.nber.org/papers/w11243)). That is a small sample. It shows that emotion and results go together, not that training your psychology makes money. The disposition effect ([Odean, 1998](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers%20current%20versions/areinvestorsreluctant.pdf)) is a large-sample example of a behavioural error, and a fixed rule removes it.

### "Keep a trading journal"

Untestable for us, and unsupported by any large-sample evidence we found. We found no study measuring whether journaling improves results. Our search quota ran out, so a study may exist that we missed. The nearest evidence is discouraging: Brazilian day traders showed no learning over 300+ days ([Chague et al.](https://ebicapital.nl/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/day-trading.pdf)). For a systematic engine, the equivalent is a logged trial register, which helps against overfitting ([Bailey et al., 2014](https://www.ams.org/notices/201405/rnoti-p458.pdf)).

### "Costs matter" (rarely stressed in discretionary courses)

Supported. Barber and Odean found that most losses at a large US discount broker came from trading costs (as summarised in [Barber, Lee, Liu and Odean, 2011](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf), page 1). Even a slow professional system costs Carver about 0.9% of capital a year ([qoppac year 12](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2026/04/annual-performance-update-year-12.html), via our earlier note). AQR argues that implementation choices "can actually matter a lot" ([Israel, Jiang and Ross, 2017](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Craftsmanship-Alpha-An-Application-to-Style-Investing)).

### Day trader studies

- Brazil: 97% of persistent day traders lost money, 1.1% beat the minimum wage, 0.5% beat a bank teller's starting salary ([Chague et al., 2020](https://ebicapital.nl/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/day-trading.pdf)).
- Taiwan: fewer than 1% predictably profitable; aggregate day trader performance negative ([Barber et al.](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/Day%20Traders/Day%20Trading%20and%20Learning%20110217.pdf)). Individual investors' total losses exceeded 2% of Taiwan's GDP in 1995 to 1999 (as cited in [Barber et al., 2011](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)).
- Warrior Trading's own disclaimer cites older US studies in which 35% to 50% of day traders were profitable ([pricing page](https://www.warriortrading.com/pricing/)). We did not check those studies.

### Prop firms

We found no prop firm that publishes an audited pass rate or payout rate. FTMO publishes only totals: 4.5M+ customers and $650M+ paid ([FTMO](https://ftmo.com/en/)). Industry pass rates of around 10% are widely repeated online (unverified, no primary source found).

### Regulator warnings about trading education

- SEC: "Remember that 'educational' seminars, classes, and books about day trading may not be objective." It also says to be wary of firms that will not disclose client profit and loss figures ([SEC, Day Trading: Your Dollars at Risk](https://www.sec.gov/about/reports-publications/investorpubsdaytipshtm)).
- FTC: the Warrior Trading action above ([press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/04/federal-trade-commission-cracks-down-warrior-trading-misleading-consumers-false-investment-promises)).
- CFTC: "AI technology can't predict the future or sudden market changes". It cites a $1.7 billion fraud that promised 10% a month from a trading bot ([CFTC advisory](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/AITradingBots.html)).
- FINRA: its intraday trading page warns that frequent trading costs "might erode your returns" ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/investors/learn-to-invest/advanced-investing/day-trading-margin-requirements-know-rules)). We did not find a FINRA page specific to trading courses (unverified, search quota ran out).

## What systematic practitioners say to learn instead

1. **Overfitting.** With enough trials, an impressive backtest is easy to find by chance ([Bailey, Borwein, Lopez de Prado and Zhu, 2014](https://www.ams.org/notices/201405/rnoti-p458.pdf)). Man AHL's academic board: "An overfitted strategy will likely underperform when faced with new data", and discretionary traders are especially prone to seeing false patterns ([Man Institute, 2015](https://www.man.com/maninstitute/overfitting-and-its-impact-on-the-investor)). Carver lists "three kinds of overfitting" among his core topics ([start page](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/p/systematic-trading-start-here.html)).
2. **Costs.** Measure them before trusting any return (see section 2). AQR treats implementation as a source of return in its own right ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Craftsmanship-Alpha-An-Application-to-Style-Investing)).
3. **Position sizing by volatility.** Carver's Starter System sizes from a risk target ([Leveraged Trading](https://www.systematicmoney.org/leveraged-trading)). Moskowitz et al. scale every position by its volatility ([TSMOM](https://w4.stern.nyu.edu/facdir/lpederse/papers/TimeSeriesMomentum.pdf)).
4. **Diversification.** The trend results that hold up are for portfolios across many markets ([Moskowitz et al.](https://w4.stern.nyu.edu/facdir/lpederse/papers/TimeSeriesMomentum.pdf)). Adding FX styles to stocks and bonds raised the out-of-sample Sharpe in [Kroencke, Schindler and Schrimpf, 2014](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/revfin/v18y2014i5p1847-1883..html) (via our earlier note).
5. **Expect decay.** Published edges shrink ([McLean and Pontiff](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12365); [Hutchinson et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.irfa.2022.102245)).

Gaps: we did not read a Winton write-up or Lopez de Prado's "10 reasons most ML funds fail" (SSRN blocked us). Chan's views are from our earlier note, not reread today.
