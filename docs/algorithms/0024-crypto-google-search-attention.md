## How it works

**Simple:** When lots of people suddenly search for 'Bitcoin' on Google, the price has tended to rise over the next couple of weeks.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like a restaurant that gets busier the week after it trends on social media.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

Construct weekly Google search volume for the coin name (de-trended) and sort weeks into quintiles. Liu and Tsyvinski find a one standard deviation rise in Bitcoin search predicts a 2.3 percent higher 2-week-ahead return.

</details>

### The rule

Each Sunday, compute the change in Google Trends search index for 'Bitcoin' versus its trailing average; hold BTC next week if the change is in the top quintile of past values (cutoffs from past data only), otherwise hold cash.

### Data needed

- Google Trends weekly search index (free)
- Daily BTC prices

### Why it might work

Attention brings new retail buyers whose demand pushes prices up with a lag, consistent with Da, Engelberg and Gao style attention effects cited by Liu and Tsyvinski.

### Why it might fail

Google Trends values are rescaled each download and revised, which can create silent lookahead. Attention spikes also come before crashes. The evidence comes from 2011-2018, mostly one bubble.
