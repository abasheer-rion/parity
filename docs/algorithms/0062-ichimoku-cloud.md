## How it works

**Simple:** Draw a shaded 'cloud' from averages of past highs and lows. Price above the cloud means up-trend, so buy; below means down-trend, so sell.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like reading weather from a cloud bank: you only go out sailing when the sky above you is clear of it.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

Ichimoku uses midpoints of high-low ranges: Tenkan (9), Kijun (26), Senkou A ((Tenkan+Kijun)/2 shifted 26 ahead), Senkou B (52-period midpoint shifted 26 ahead); the space between A and B is the cloud. Retail rules buy when price closes above the cloud and Tenkan crosses above Kijun. Lim, Yanyali and Savidge (2016) argue it behaves like a momentum and breakout strategy with a natural stop loss.

</details>

### The rule

Daily: long when close > max(Senkou A, Senkou B) and Tenkan(9) > Kijun(26); short when close < min(Senkou A, Senkou B) and Tenkan < Kijun; exit when price closes back inside the cloud.

### Data needed

- Daily high, low and close (FX OHLC from a free source such as Yahoo or Stooq; FRED gives closes only)

### Why it might work

Same trend logic as breakouts. Lim, Yanyali and Savidge (2016) say returns are positively skewed with a small left tail because of the built-in stop.

### Why it might fail

The only test we found is on single stocks in Japan and the US over 2005 to 2014, and we could only read the abstract. The 9/26/52 settings come from a six-day trading week and were never tuned out of sample.
