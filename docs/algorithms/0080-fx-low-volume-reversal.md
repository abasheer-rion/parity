## How it works

**Simple:** When a currency pair moves a lot on a quiet day with little trading, the move often reverses the next day. Buy the pairs that fell on low volume and sell the pairs that rose on low volume.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like a price shout in an empty market hall: with few people around, one big order moves the price, and it drifts back once more traders arrive.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

Each day sort 31 pairs into three groups by previous 24-hour return, then into three by abnormal CLS volume. Long the low-return, low-volume portfolio; short the high-return, low-volume portfolio. Hold one day.

</details>

### The rule

Daily: rank pairs by yesterday's return and by yesterday's volume relative to normal. Among low-volume pairs, buy yesterday's losers and sell yesterday's winners. Close after one day.

### Data needed

- Daily FX volume by pair (CLS data, paid)
- Daily returns and bid-ask spreads

### Why it might work

Price moves on thin volume are more likely to reflect temporary liquidity pressure than news, so they tend to reverse.

### Why it might fail

Daily turnover makes costs decisive: the Sharpe ratio fell from 1.70 to just over 0.90 after costs. The volume data are paid, and the sample (2011-2017) is short.
