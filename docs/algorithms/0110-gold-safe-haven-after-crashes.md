## How it works

**Simple:** Gold tends not to fall when stocks crash, but only for a couple of weeks. After an extreme down day in stocks, move some money into gold, then move it back after about 15 trading days.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like an umbrella that works during a downpour but not for the rest of the week.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

Regressing gold returns on stock returns, with extra terms for days when stock returns are in their lowest 10%, 5% or 1%, Baur and Lucey find gold is uncorrelated with stocks on average (a hedge) and not negatively affected on extreme down days (a safe haven) in the US, UK and Germany. In their portfolio analysis the protection lasts only about 15 trading days.

</details>

### The rule

Our simple version, for testing: when the S&P 500's daily return is below its rolling 1st percentile, switch a fixed share of the stock holding into gold for 15 trading days, then switch back. This rule is ours.

### Data needed

- Daily gold price (no free daily source in our catalog)
- Daily stock index returns

### Why it might work

Flight to quality: in panics some investors sell stocks and buy an asset with no credit risk, which supports gold briefly.

### Why it might fail

The sample is one decade (1995 to 2005) with few extreme days. Gold can also fall in a crash when investors sell everything for cash. Fifteen days is an average, not a rule.
