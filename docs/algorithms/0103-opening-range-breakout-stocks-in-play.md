## How it works

**Simple:** Watch only the stocks with unusual trading that morning, usually because of news. If the first five minutes close up, buy; if down, sell short; get out by the close.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like joining the crowd that has already started running, but only on days when there is a real reason to run.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

Stocks in play are screened by relative volume (opening price above $5, average volume of at least 1 million shares over 14 days, 14-day ATR above $0.50, per one summary). Trade in the direction of the first 5-minute candle, with a stop sized from ATR, and close at the end of the day. The paper holds the top 20 stocks by relative volume each day.

</details>

### The rule

Each day, rank liquid US stocks by opening relative volume and take the top 20. Go long if the first 5-minute bar closes above its open, short if below; stop-loss sized from the 14-day ATR (the exact fraction is in the paper, which we have not read); exit at the close.

### Data needed

- 5-minute bars for all US stocks
- Daily volume and ATR

### Why it might work

News days bring informed order flow and attention, so the opening move tends to continue during the day for stocks with real news.

### Why it might fail

One of the authors sells day-trading education, and the paper's 2016 to 2023 sample is short. Intraday costs and short-sale constraints on news stocks are hard to model, and later costs could take most of the edge.
