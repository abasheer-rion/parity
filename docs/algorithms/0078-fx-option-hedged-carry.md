## How it works

**Simple:** Do the carry trade, and each month buy insurance in the form of currency options that pay out if the trade suddenly crashes.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like a fruit seller who pays a little each month for crop insurance so one bad storm cannot wipe out the business.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

Equal-weight carry across 25 currencies against USD, monthly, with at-the-money options bought on each position. Caballero and Doyle report the hedged trade earned a higher Sharpe ratio than the unhedged one over 2004-2012. Jurek (2014) finds crash risk premia explain at most one-third of carry returns.

</details>

### The rule

Monthly: long high-rate and short low-rate currencies against USD, equal weights. For each position buy a one-month at-the-money option that protects against the position's loss. Hold to expiry and repeat.

### Data needed

- FX option implied volatilities or prices by currency (not free)
- Monthly spot and forward rates

### Why it might work

If FX options were priced fairly for crash risk, hedged carry should earn little. Both papers find hedged carry kept most of its return, suggesting carry pays for something other than currency crash risk.

### Why it might fail

Option data are expensive and the 2004-2012 sample is short and includes 2008. Option bid-ask spreads can be wide for smaller currencies.
