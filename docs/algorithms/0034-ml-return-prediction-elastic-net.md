## How it works

**Simple:** You give a computer many facts about each stock and the economy, and it learns which combinations predict next month's return, with rules that stop it from memorizing noise.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like a teacher who grades on many small clues but is forced to ignore clues that only worked once.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

Forecast monthly excess returns from 94 stock characteristics, their interactions with 8 macro predictors, and industry dummies, comparing OLS, elastic net, PCR, PLS, random forests, boosted trees and neural nets. Train 1957-1974, validate 1975-1986, test 1987-2016 with annual refits.

</details>

### The rule

Each month, fit the model on an expanding window, predict next-month returns for all stocks, and hold a value-weighted long top decile minus short bottom decile portfolio; for elastic net, tune lambda and the L1/L2 mix on the validation window.

### Data needed

- Monthly stock returns and characteristics (CRSP/Compustat or open versions)
- Macro predictors (dividend yield, term spread, default spread, etc.)
- T-bill rate

### Why it might work

Many weak predictors combine nonlinearly; shrinkage and nonlinearity reduce overfitting compared with OLS on hundreds of signals (Gu, Kelly and Xiu).

### Why it might fail

Elastic net's out-of-sample R2 was only 0.11 percent per month for individual stocks. Much of the long-short profit may come from small, illiquid stocks, and the paper reports Sharpe ratios before trading costs.

### After publication

Later studies (for example Avramov, Cheng and Metzker, Management Science 2023) report that ML gains shrink a lot once microcaps, distressed firms and costly trades are excluded; that paper was not re-read for this entry.
