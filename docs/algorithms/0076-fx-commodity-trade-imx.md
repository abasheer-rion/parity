# Commodity exporters minus finished-goods exporters (IMX)

## In plain words

Countries that sell raw materials have riskier money than countries that sell finished goods. Buy the currencies of raw-material sellers and sell the currencies of finished-goods sellers.

## How it works

**Simple:** Countries that sell raw materials have riskier money than countries that sell finished goods. Buy the currencies of raw-material sellers and sell the currencies of finished-goods sellers.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like a farmer and a baker: when hard times come, the farmer's income swings more, so you ask more to lend to the farmer.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

Signal = (net exports of basic goods + net imports of finished goods) / total trade, averaged over the prior four years. Long the highest-ranked currencies, short the lowest. Rankings update yearly; returns are monthly.

</details>

### The rule

Each year compute each country's trade ratio from the past four years of trade data. Long the top portfolio (commodity exporters), short the bottom portfolio (finished-goods exporters), equal weights, via forwards. Rebalance yearly, roll monthly.

### Data needed

- Bilateral trade by product type (UN Comtrade, free)
- Monthly spot and forward rates

### Why it might work

Ready, Roussanov and Ward model commodity countries as more exposed to global productivity shocks, so their currencies earn a premium and also pay high interest.

### Why it might fail

The return overlaps with carry. The sample ends in 2012, before the commodity bust of 2014-2016, and the commodity-currency link may have changed after 2020 (see RES-0001 on Rees, 2025).
