## How it works

**Simple:** A neural network with memory reads the last 240 days of returns for each stock and guesses whether it will beat the others tomorrow.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like a coach who watches each player's last season of games to guess who will score in the next match.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

Train an LSTM on sequences of 240 standardized daily returns for S&P 500 constituents to classify whether next-day return exceeds the cross-sectional median; go long the top k and short the bottom k each day. Gains are partly explained by a short-term reversal pattern in volatile stocks.

</details>

### The rule

Each day, rank S&P 500 constituents by the LSTM's predicted probability of beating the median tomorrow; buy the top 10 and short the bottom 10 equally weighted at the close and hold one day; retrain every 250 trading days on the prior 750 days.

### Data needed

- Daily total returns of index constituents with historical membership lists
- Transaction cost assumption (5 bps per half-turn in the paper)

### Why it might work

Nonlinear models can pick up short-term reversal and volatility patterns that linear models miss (Fischer and Krauss).

### Why it might fail

The paper itself shows the edge deteriorating after 2010, with the LSTM roughly flat after costs over 2010-2015. Daily long-short on 20 stocks has high turnover, and a pre-cost Sharpe of 5.8 is typical of short-term reversal signals that do not survive realistic costs.

### After publication

Within the paper's own sample, returns faded after 2010. Later Kaggle competitions (Jane Street Market Prediction 2021, Real-Time Market Data Forecasting 2024-25) used GRU/LSTM and tree ensembles, but their scores and live results were not verified for this entry.
