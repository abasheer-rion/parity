## How it works

**Simple:** Instead of investing all your money at once, you put in the same amount every month. You buy more when prices are low and less when they are high.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like filling your car with the same 40 dollars of gas every week no matter the price per gallon.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

Split a lump sum into equal tranches invested at fixed intervals. Because markets drift upward on average, holding cash while waiting lowers expected return; DCA mainly reduces regret and timing risk rather than raising returns.

</details>

### The rule

Invest 1/k of the available cash into the target asset (for example an S&P 500 ETF or BTC) on the first trading day of each month for k months, holding the rest in cash; compare against investing everything on day one.

### Data needed

- Monthly total-return index of the target asset
- Cash or T-bill rate

### Why it might work

No return edge is claimed; Vanguard frames it as a behavioral tool that lowers the chance of investing everything just before a fall.

### Why it might fail

Vanguard finds lump sum beat cost averaging 61.6 to 73.7 percent of the time across markets from 1976 to 2022, because the uninvested cash misses the equity premium.
