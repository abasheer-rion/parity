## How it works

**Simple:** VIX futures usually cost more than the VIX itself and drift down towards it as they expire. Sell them when that gap is large, buy them when it is upside down, and hedge with S&P 500 futures.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like selling umbrellas at a markup on a cloudy day that usually stays dry.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

The daily roll is (front VIX future minus VIX) divided by business days to settlement. Short one front VIX future when the roll is above 0.10 points (contango), long when it is below -0.10 (backwardation), hold five business days, and hedge market exposure with mini S&P 500 futures using out-of-sample hedge ratios.

</details>

### The rule

Each day after 3:00 pm Chicago time: if the front VIX future (the nearest contract with at least 10 business days to settlement) is in contango with a daily roll above 0.10 points, short it and buy mini S&P 500 futures as a hedge; if in backwardation with a daily roll below -0.10, buy it and sell the hedge; exit after 5 business days.

### Data needed

- Daily VIX futures settlement prices by contract
- VIX index
- E-mini S&P 500 futures

### Why it might work

Investors pay a premium to hold VIX futures as crash insurance, so the futures sit above the expected future VIX and sellers collect the difference (Simon and Campasano argue the basis is a risk premium, not a forecast).

### Why it might fail

Short volatility loses suddenly and heavily. On 5 February 2018 the VIX more than doubled in a day and the XIV short-VIX note was terminated after losing almost all its value. The sample (2006 to 2011) is short.
