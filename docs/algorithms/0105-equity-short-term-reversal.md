# Short-term reversal in stocks (one-month losers beat winners)

## In plain words

Buy the stocks that fell most last month and sell the ones that rose most; over the next month they tend to swap back.

## How it works

**Simple:** Buy the stocks that fell most last month and sell the ones that rose most; over the next month they tend to swap back.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like a crowded doorway: people pushed out of the way last month drift back in.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

Monthly returns of individual stocks are negatively related to the previous month's return (and positively to returns 12 months back). Sorting stocks into deciles on a forecast built from these patterns gives a large spread in next-month returns. The usual explanation is that liquidity providers are paid for absorbing order imbalances.

</details>

### The rule

At each month end, rank stocks by the past month's return; buy the bottom decile and sell the top decile for one month.

### Data needed

- Monthly stock returns (the Kenneth French data library has a free short-term reversal factor)

### Why it might work

Prices overshoot when traders demand liquidity, and the investors who absorb that demand are paid when prices bounce back.

### Why it might fail

Turnover is close to 100% a month, so trading costs eat much of the profit, especially in small stocks. Part of the effect is bid-ask bounce rather than a tradable return.
