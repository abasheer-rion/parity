## How it works

**Simple:** Own a little of the money of about 20 emerging countries, equally, against the US dollar, and collect their higher interest.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like an index fund, but for the currencies of fast-growing countries.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

Equal-weighted long position in one-month forwards (or spot plus interest gap) on about 20 EM currencies against USD, rebalanced monthly and rolled with FX swaps. Gilmore and Hayashi report a Sharpe ratio of 1.34 over January 1999 to September 2008 and estimate rolling costs of about 30 basis points a year.

</details>

### The rule

Monthly: equal weight long every EM currency with a price and a rate, funded in USD; total gross exposure 1.

### Data needed

- Daily EM spot rates (ECB reference rates)
- EM short-term interest rates
- US 3-month rate

### Why it might work

EM currencies paid a large interest premium and, in the authors' sample, did not depreciate enough to offset it; diversification across regions kept volatility low.

### Why it might fail

The sample (1999 to 2008) was an EM boom with a falling dollar and ends a month before the 2008 crash; the authors worked for or consulted for Banque AIG and used proprietary data.
