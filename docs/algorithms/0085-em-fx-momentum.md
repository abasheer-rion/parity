## How it works

**Simple:** Among emerging market currencies, buy the ones that rose most over the past few months and sell the ones that fell most.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like backing the players on a hot streak in a league where few scouts are watching.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

Rank currencies by past excess return over a formation window (1 to 12 months), long winners, short losers, hold one month. Menkhoff et al. find spreads up to about 10% a year before costs across up to 48 currencies, falling to about 4% after full spreads for the best version, and basically nothing among developed currencies after costs.

</details>

### The rule

Monthly: rank EM currencies by past 3-month return, long the top third, short the bottom third, equal weights, hold one month.

### Data needed

- Daily EM spot rates (ECB reference rates)
- EM short-term interest rates for carry credit

### Why it might work

Limits to arbitrage: high costs and country risk in minor currencies keep slow-moving trends from being traded away.

### Why it might fail

The return lives where costs are highest, so a realistic spread may consume it; the developed-market version already failed in our tests (EXP-0004).
