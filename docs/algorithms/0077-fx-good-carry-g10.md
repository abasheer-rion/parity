## How it works

**Simple:** The usual carry trade buys the five G10 currencies paying the most interest and sells the five paying the least. This version leaves out a few currencies, like the Australian dollar and the yen, that made the trade crash-prone in the past.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like dropping the two players who keep fouling: the team scores a bit less but gets fewer red cards.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

Standard G10 carry: long the five highest forward premia, short the five lowest, equal weights, monthly. The good carry variants drop currencies, mostly AUD, JPY and NOK, chosen because removing them raised the past Sharpe ratio. The authors warn the selection uses hindsight.

</details>

### The rule

Monthly: rank G10 currencies by one-month forward premium against USD. Long the top five, short the bottom five, equal weights. Good carry variant: exclude the currencies the paper drops, or pick exclusions with a rolling past-Sharpe rule to avoid hindsight.

### Data needed

- Monthly G10 spot and forward rates with bid and ask

### Why it might work

A few funding and investment currencies drive most carry crashes; leaving them out may keep the premium with less negative skew.

### Why it might fail

The authors themselves note look-back bias: the excluded currencies were chosen with knowledge of the full sample. A rolling version that chooses exclusions only from past data is the honest test.
