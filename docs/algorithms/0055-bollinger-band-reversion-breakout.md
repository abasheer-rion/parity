## How it works

**Simple:** Draw a band two 'normal wiggles' above and below the 20-day average. Touching the bottom band means buy, touching the top means sell. When the band gets very narrow, wait for price to burst out and follow it.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like a dog on a leash: most of the time it stays near you, but when it suddenly pulls hard after being still, it is usually chasing something.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

Middle band = SMA(20); upper and lower bands = SMA(20) plus or minus 2 rolling standard deviations. Reversion version: buy a close below the lower band, sell a close above the upper band, exit at the middle band. Squeeze version: when band width hits a multi-month low, trade in the direction of the first close outside the bands. Lento, Gradojevic and Wright (2007) test the reversion and contrarian forms.

</details>

### The rule

Daily: m = SMA20(close), s = 20-day standard deviation. Reversion: long when close < m - 2s, short when close > m + 2s, exit when close crosses m. Breakout: if (4s/m) is at a 120-day low, go long on the first close above m + 2s (short below m - 2s), exit on a close back through m.

### Data needed

- Daily closing prices (SPY, FX spot)
- Cost estimate

### Why it might work

Reversion assumes short-term overreaction; the squeeze assumes calm periods are followed by large moves (volatility clustering). No risk premium is claimed.

### Why it might fail

Lento, Gradojevic and Wright (2007) find the bands are 'consistently unable to earn profits in excess of the buy-and-hold' after costs, though a contrarian use did better. The two versions give opposite signals at the same band touch, so a trader can always find one that 'worked' in hindsight.
