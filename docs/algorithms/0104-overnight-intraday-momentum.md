## How it works

**Simple:** Split each stock's daily return into what happens while the market is shut (close to next open) and while it is open. Stocks that did well overnight last month tend to keep doing well overnight and badly during the day.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like two teams pulling a rope at different times of day: each wins its own shift, and the rope barely moves overall.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

Sort stocks each month by last month's overnight return; the value-weighted top-minus-bottom decile earns a large positive overnight alpha and a large negative intraday alpha next month. Momentum profits are earned overnight; many other strategies earn theirs intraday. The authors link this to individuals trading near the open and institutions trading during the day.

</details>

### The rule

Each month, rank stocks by last month's summed overnight returns; hold the top decile long and the bottom decile short only overnight (buy at the close, sell at the next open).

### Data needed

- Daily open and close prices for all US stocks (CRSP in the paper)

### Why it might work

Different investor groups trade at different times of day, and their opposing demands persist, so each part of the return is predictable on its own.

### Why it might fail

Trading twice a day costs a lot, and the profits sit in the gap between close and open, where spreads are wide. The close-to-close return of the combined strategy is small because the two parts offset.
