## How it works

**Simple:** You place many buy orders below the price and sell orders above it, like rungs on a ladder. Every time the price bounces up and down between rungs, you make a small profit.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like a shop that buys umbrellas cheap on sunny days and sells them dear on rainy days, as long as the weather keeps changing.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

Place geometric or arithmetic buy and sell limit orders at fixed intervals around a center price; each round trip earns the grid step minus fees. Chen et al. show that under simple random-walk assumptions the expected return of a static grid is essentially zero; profits from oscillation are offset by inventory losses when price leaves the range.

</details>

### The rule

Choose a range [L, U] and N levels with ratio step g; buy one unit each time price falls through a level and sell one unit each time it rises through the level above. The dynamic version resets the grid around the current price when price exits the range.

### Data needed

- 1-minute OHLC candles (Binance or OKX public API)
- Maker fee schedule

### Why it might work

Grids sell volatility: they profit when price mean-reverts inside a range. There is no clear risk premium; Chen et al. show the static grid has near-zero expectation before fees.

### Why it might fail

When price trends out of the range the grid is left holding a falling asset or all cash in a rally. Fees eat small grids. The dynamic grid results come from a parameter sweep over one bull-heavy sample (2021-2024), so the best cells are in-sample picks.
