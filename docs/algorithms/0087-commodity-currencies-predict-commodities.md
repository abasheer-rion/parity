## How it works

**Simple:** When the Australian, Canadian and New Zealand dollars rise, commodity prices tend to rise in the following months. So use those currencies as a signal to own or sell commodities.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like reading the odds to guess the result: the currency market prices in news about demand before commodity prices catch up.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

Regress next-quarter changes in commodity price indices on this quarter's changes in commodity-currency exchange rates. Chen et al. find robust out-of-sample predictability over samples ending in 2008, and that the reverse direction (commodities predicting currencies) is notably less robust, consistent with exchange rates being forward looking.

</details>

### The rule

Monthly: average the past 3-month returns of AUD, CAD and NZD against USD; hold energy futures long when positive and short when negative, volatility-scaled.

### Data needed

- Daily AUD, CAD, NZD spot (FRED H.10)
- Energy futures contracts 1 and 2 (EIA)

### Why it might work

Exchange rates are asset prices that react to expected future commodity demand; spot commodity prices respond to current supply and demand imbalances.

### Why it might fail

The paper's gains are statistical (lower forecast error), not trading returns; the link may have changed after 2008; energy futures are only part of the commodity indices the paper forecasts.

### After publication

Ferraro, Rogoff and Rossi (2015) find the oil and Canadian dollar link is mostly same-day and absent at monthly and quarterly frequency, consistent with this entry's direction.
