## How it works

**Simple:** The old London afternoon gold price was set by a few banks on a private call. Prices in the futures market started moving in the direction of the result within minutes of the call starting, before it was announced.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like a sealed-bid auction where the bidders' friends keep placing side bets while the envelopes are still being opened.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

Using minute data for COMEX gold futures (GC) and the GLD fund from 2007 to 2012, volume and volatility jump right after the PM fixing starts, informed trades in the first four minutes earn significant returns, and early trades predict the fixing's direction, in some cases more than 90% of the time. The published result has no effect.

</details>

### The rule

At the start of the London PM fixing (3 pm London), trade gold futures in the direction of the first minutes' price move and exit when the fixing result is published.

### Data needed

- Minute-level gold futures prices (paid)
- Fixing start and end times

### Why it might work

Banks on the fixing call knew order imbalances before the public, and that information leaked into exchange prices.

### Why it might fail

The fixing was replaced by an electronic, administered auction (the LBMA Gold Price) in 2015 after manipulation fines, so the leak this exploited should no longer exist. It needed minute-level execution.
