## How it works

**Simple:** You keep offering to buy a little below the price and sell a little above it, earning the gap. If you end up holding too much, you shift your prices to get rid of it.

<details><summary>Analogy</summary>

Like a currency exchange booth at an airport that buys and sells at different rates and changes its rates if it runs low on euros.

</details>

<details><summary>Precise</summary>

Quote around a reservation price r = s - q*gamma*sigma^2*(T - t) that leans against inventory q, with an optimal total spread gamma*sigma^2*(T - t) + (2/gamma)*ln(1 + gamma/kappa). Gamma is risk aversion, sigma volatility, kappa the order-arrival decay with distance from mid.

</details>

### The rule

Every tick, compute mid s, inventory q and recent volatility sigma; post bid at r - delta/2 and ask at r + delta/2 using the formulas above with chosen gamma and kappa, cancel and replace when the quote drifts beyond a tolerance, and stop at the end of the session.

### Data needed

- Level 2 order book and trade ticks
- Maker and taker fees, rebates
- Latency to the exchange

### Why it might work

Market makers earn the bid-ask spread as payment for providing liquidity and bearing inventory risk.

### Why it might fail

Retail bots face adverse selection from faster traders and pay fees that professional makers avoid. In Falces Marin et al. (2022) the tuned Avellaneda-Stoikov baseline had negative mean daily Sharpe on 30 BTC-USD test days.
