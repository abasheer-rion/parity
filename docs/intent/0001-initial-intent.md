# Initial intent

## In plain words

We want to find out if a computer program that trades money from different countries can do better than the simplest thing anyone can do: buy a slice of the 500 biggest US companies and wait. We think it probably cannot, and that is fine. Either way we learn something, and maybe write a paper about it.

## Summary

Parity is a research project by Ayan, Amanda and Abanish. We want to find out, honestly and with evidence, whether a systematic strategy built around currencies can beat the S&P 500. It is a proof of concept, not a business. If the answer is no, we lose nothing except time and come away understanding markets better. If the answer is yes, or partly yes, we would like to write it up as a paper.

This page records what we set out to do on 2026-10-20, in our own words, before any results came in. We will not edit it to fit later findings. If our aims change, we will add a new intent document that supersedes this one.

## What we want

1. **A clear answer to one question.** Can a currency strategy, possibly informed by commodity prices and macro news, beat the S&P 500 after costs and on a fair, risk-matched basis? What we mean by "beat" is pinned down in [DEC-0006](../decisions/0006-how-we-define-beating-the-sp-500.md).
2. **Something different from the usual retail bot.** The space is crowded. We would rather test one idea that is well grounded in economics and done carefully than copy a popular indicator strategy. Our first candidate is the link between commodity exporters and their currencies ([HYP-0003](../hypotheses/0003-commodity-currencies-catch-up.md)), which also gives us a way into commodities.
3. **Strategies we can swap out.** The engine should treat each strategy as a plug-in that can be removed at any time to make room for the next one ([DEC-0003](../decisions/0003-strategies-are-plugins.md)).
4. **Our own historical data.** Scrapers that pull free historical data for currencies, interest rates, commodities and the S&P 500, cached so experiments are repeatable ([REF-0003](../reference/0003-data-sources.md)).
5. **Simulations.** Backtests that anyone on the team can rerun and get the same numbers, with costs and carry included.
6. **A dashboard of leading indicators.** Not only "did it make money", but signals that tell us where to look next and where a model needs more work ([REF-0002](../reference/0002-kpis.md)).
7. **Grounding in research.** Read the papers, study existing open-source algorithms and public results (including crypto bots, where many people publish what they tried), read what practitioners say in forums, and compare all of it against our own results ([RES-0001](../research/0001-reading-list.md), [RES-0002](../research/0002-open-source-and-public-results.md), [RES-0003](../research/0003-forums-and-practitioners.md)).
8. **A record we can learn from.** Every decision, experiment and session is written down and linked, so that six months from now we can see what we tried, why, and what happened ([DEC-0001](../decisions/0001-keep-a-decision-record.md)).
9. **Free.** Everything runs inside free tiers: GitHub, Supabase and Cloudflare Pages ([DEC-0002](../decisions/0002-free-tier-stack.md), [REF-0005](../reference/0005-free-tier-budget.md)).

## What we do not want

- **Not a product, and no real money for now.** Regulation is out of scope at this stage because nothing is offered to anyone and nothing trades live.
- **Not a black box we cannot explain.** If a result looks good, we want to know why before we believe it.
- **Not microsecond trading, at least not yet.** Reacting to gas prices or news within microseconds needs co-located servers and paid data feeds. We will study event-driven ideas at the speeds free data allows and treat true low latency as out of reach for this proof of concept ([DEC-0005](../decisions/0005-no-microsecond-trading-in-the-poc.md)).
- **Not a backtest that only looks good because we tried many things.** We will count our trials and report deflated statistics.

## How we will know

The project has succeeded as research if, for each idea we test, we can say with evidence whether it works, how confident we are, and why. It has succeeded as a strategy only if something passes every test in [DEC-0006](../decisions/0006-how-we-define-beating-the-sp-500.md) on data it was not tuned on.

The first baseline run is in [EXP-0001](../experiments/0001-baseline-backtests.md). None of the three starting strategies beat the S&P 500, which is what most of the literature would predict and is a useful place to start from.
