# KPIs and how to read them

## In plain words

The scorecard. Every number we use to judge a strategy, what it means, and what a good or bad value looks like. If a word here is new, the glossary explains it.

## Summary

We track three groups of numbers. **Outcome** numbers say whether a strategy made money and how painfully. **Robustness** numbers say whether we should believe it. **Leading indicators** say whether a signal has information at all, before portfolio rules and costs, and so where to spend effort next. The code is in `engine/src/parity/metrics/`. If a definition here changes, the code changes in the same pull request.

## The benchmark

The whole US stock market with dividends reinvested, held without trading and without costs, over exactly the same dates as each strategy. It is built from the Fama and French market return and extended to the latest day with the S&P 500 index ([DEC-0008](../decisions/0008-benchmark-data-without-yahoo.md)). It tracks the S&P 500 closely: daily correlation with SPY of about 0.98 and a yearly return about 0.1 to 0.4 points higher since 1999, mostly because it includes smaller companies and pays no fund fee. What counts as beating it is set in [DEC-0006](../decisions/0006-how-we-define-beating-the-sp-500.md).

## Outcome

| KPI | Definition | Read it as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CAGR | Compound annual growth rate of the net equity curve. | Return per year. Only comparable at equal volatility. |
| Volatility | Standard deviation of daily returns times the square root of 252. | Risk per year. |
| Sharpe | Mean daily return over its standard deviation, times the square root of 252. Zero risk-free rate. | Return per unit of risk. The main comparison. |
| Sortino | Like Sharpe, but only downside days count as risk. | Penalises crashes more than rallies. |
| Max drawdown | Largest fall from a previous peak. | The worst loss you would have sat through. |
| Calmar | CAGR divided by the absolute max drawdown. | Return per unit of worst pain. |
| Hit rate | Share of trading days with a positive return. | Low hit rates are fine if winners are larger. |
| Yearly returns | Return in each calendar year. | Shows whether results come from one lucky year. |

## Robustness

| KPI | Definition | Read it as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deflated Sharpe | Probability that the true Sharpe is above zero, given the number of strategies tried, skew and fat tails (Bailey and Lopez de Prado, 2014). | Below 0.95 means it could be luck. |
| Break-even cost | Average gross daily return divided by average daily turnover, in basis points. | The cost per trade at which the strategy earns nothing. Should be several times real spreads (about 0.5 to 2 bp for G10 majors). |
| Turnover per year | Sum of absolute changes in weights, per year. | How much it trades. High turnover magnifies any cost error. |
| Excess CAGR | Strategy CAGR minus SPY CAGR. | Positive is needed, at matched risk. |
| Information ratio | Mean of strategy minus SPY return, over its volatility, annualised. | How consistently it beats SPY. |
| Beta and correlation | Sensitivity to and co-movement with SPY. | Low or negative means it could diversify a stock portfolio. |

On the roadmap: probability of backtest overfitting (Bailey, Borwein, Lopez de Prado and Zhu, 2016) and sub-period stability.

## Leading indicators

These tell us where to look and where a model needs more work.

| KPI | Definition | Read it as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information coefficient (IC) | Rank correlation between a strategy's raw signal and the return that follows, for each date across instruments. | Above about 0.02 to 0.03 is meaningful for daily FX. Negative means the signal points the wrong way. |
| IC decay | IC measured at 1, 5, 21 and 63 days ahead. | Where the information sits in time. If IC rises with horizon, trade slower. If it is highest at one day and gone by five, speed matters. |
| IC t-stat | Mean IC over its standard deviation, times the square root of the count. | Overlapping horizons inflate this; treat values under about 3 with care. |
| Exposure | Latest weights by instrument. | What the strategy is betting on right now. |
| Data coverage | First and last date of every series, and when it was fetched. | A stale or broken series quietly ruins results. |

With fewer than three instruments, a cross-sectional IC is not meaningful, so we pool instruments and compute one IC per calendar year.
