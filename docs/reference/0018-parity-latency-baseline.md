# Parity latency baseline

## In plain words

The first measurement of how old our data is and how long each step takes, taken on 2026-10-25 when latency tracking started ([DEC-0045](../decisions/0045-we-track-how-old-our-data-is-at-every-hop-and-how-fast-pages-get-it.md)). Later numbers on the Latency page can be compared with these. The slowest hop by far is the wait between refreshes: the "hourly" job ran about every three and a half hours.

## Summary

| Hop | p50 | p95 | n |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Source's as-of time to our fetch, all series | 5.1 days | 116 days | 273 |
| Scheduled start to actual start, hourly News job | 24 min | 50 min | 7 runs |
| Start to start of the hourly News job (60 min intended) | 3.5 h | 5.6 h | 6 gaps |
| News job, start to end | 2.3 min | 4.5 min | 7 runs |
| Deploy job, start to end | 6.5 min | 7.3 min | 50 runs |
| News job start to the end of the deploy it triggered | 9.2 min | 10.8 min | 6 |
| Nightly research run, scheduled start to actual start | 2.4 h | 2.4 h | 1 run |
| Our fetch to the site publishing it, live tables after the first deploy | 3 min | 8 min | 282 |
| Time to first data in a browser, front page | 274 ms | 365 ms | 3 loads |
| One data request in a browser (`/stats.json`) | 87 ms | 115 ms | 3 loads |
| Largest paint in a browser, front page | 274 ms | 274 ms | 3 loads |

Server numbers come from 26 hours of GitHub runs (2026-10-24 00:00 to 2026-10-25 02:00 UTC) and one full fetch of every source. The publish hop and the browser rows come from the live tables right after the first production deploy (2026-10-25 03:20 UTC). The three browser loads were our own test loads of the public front page from one machine in North America, so treat them as a smoke test; the signed-in apps had no beacons yet. Compare later numbers with the Latency page, not with these three.

## Data age by source

Age is how old the newest value is when fetched. Cadence is the age we expect at most; past it a row is late, past twice it stale.

| Source | Series | Median age | Oldest | Cadence | Late | Stale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FRED | 84 | 9.1 days | 297 days | 6 days (daily), 11 (H.10), 120 (monthly) | 1 | 3 |
| ECB | 36 | 2.1 days | 3.1 days | 4 days | 0 | 0 |
| US Treasury | 13 | 2.1 days | 2.1 days | 4 days | 0 | 0 |
| BIS | 32 | 6.1 days | 25 days | 21 days | 1 | 0 |
| CFTC | 46 | 5.1 days | 5.1 days | 15 days | 0 | 0 |
| French library | 20 | 55 days | 55 days | 75 days | 0 | 0 |
| Built from others | 31 | 2.1 days | 4.1 days | 4 days | 1 | 0 |
| CoinGecko | 3 | 24 min | 24 min | 7.5 h from our fetch | 0 | 0 |
| Hyperliquid | 2 | 24 min | 24 min | 2.5 h from our fetch | 0 | 0 |
| SEC | 4 | 24 min | 24 min | 25.5 h from our fetch | 0 | 0 |
| News feeds | 11 | 18 min | 18 min | 1.5 h from our fetch | 0 | 0 |
| EIA | 0 | n/a | n/a | 11 days | n/a | n/a |

- EIA series were skipped: the shared job has no EIA key.
- Fetch times for news feeds started with this change, so only 1 of the 11 feeds had one at baseline; the other 10 show as "no fetch yet" until the next hourly run.
- The three stale FRED series are the OECD interbank rates for the euro and the pound (newest value January 2026) and commercial real estate prices. A fetch cannot fix these; the source has not published.
- The search and sims services report every 5 and about 22 minutes; their health line was live at the time.

## How to compare later

- Open the Latency page in the research app (Data, then Latency), or run `parity latency freshness` and `parity latency actions --hours 26` in `engine/`.
- Compare p50 and p95 per hop with the tables above. The refresh gap should fall towards an hour now that the sims service starts a skipped refresh itself.
