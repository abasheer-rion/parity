Risk notice

Trading can lose money, sometimes more than you put in. Parity shows research about the past and simulations of what might happen. Neither tells you what will happen.

Parity is not investment advice. It does not know your situation and does not recommend anything. Before you trade, make sure you understand the risks, and consider talking to a licensed adviser.

Backtests are hypothetical

Most results on Parity are backtests: a strategy's rules applied to past prices, as if someone had followed them. No real money was traded.

  • Past results are not a guide to the future. Markets change. A strategy that worked before can stop working, or lose money.
  • Hindsight creeps in. Rules can be tuned, often by accident, to fit the past. We run tests to catch this, and we count every try, but no test catches everything.
  • Costs are estimates. We subtract estimated trading costs. Real costs, delays and slippage can be higher, especially in fast or thin markets.
  • Missing history. Some data leaves out assets that no longer exist, which can make the past look better than it was.
  • Small samples. A few good years can be luck. We show how confident each result is, and many results are "not proven".

Simulations are not forecasts

Sim World and the scenario tool show possible paths after an event, based on what happened after similar events in the past and on simple models. They show a range of outcomes, not a prediction. The real outcome can fall outside that range.

Practice mode is pretend

Practice mode uses pretend money in your browser. Real trading feels different and costs more, and real orders may not fill at the prices shown.

Crypto

  • Prices can rise or fall very fast, and can drop close to zero.
  • Markets trade all day, every day, so big moves can happen while you sleep.
  • Exchanges and wallets can be hacked, fail or freeze withdrawals. Crypto is usually not covered by deposit insurance or investor protection schemes.
  • Stablecoins can lose their peg.
  • Rules for crypto differ by country and can change suddenly.

Perpetual futures and leverage

  • Leverage multiplies losses as well as gains. A small price move against you can wipe out your margin.
  • Liquidation. If your margin runs low, the venue can close your position automatically at a bad price.
  • Funding payments. Perpetual futures charge or pay a funding rate, often every few hours. Over time it can cost a lot.
  • Venue risk. Many perpetual futures venues are offshore and not regulated where you live. People in the United States may only trade them on venues registered with the CFTC.
  • You can lose more than your first deposit on some products and venues.

ETFs

  • An ETF can trade at a price different from the value of what it holds, and may not track its index closely.
  • Leveraged and inverse ETFs reset every day. Over longer periods their returns can be very different from what you expect, even when the index ends where it started.
  • New and niche ETFs can be hard to sell at a fair price.

Currencies and commodities

Currency and commodity prices can jump on news, central bank decisions, wars and supply shocks. Trading them usually involves leverage, with the risks above.

Data limits

  • Prices can be delayed, incomplete or wrong. Free data sources sometimes contain errors, gaps or bad ticks.
  • Some prices on Parity are end of day, not live. Do not use them to time trades.
  • Each data source keeps its own licence. Some data may not be used for trading or redistribution.
  • We check data, but we cannot promise it is accurate or complete.
