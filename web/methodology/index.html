Verify methodology

How Parity Verify judges a trading record: the order of the verdicts, every check and its threshold, the luck check, trust levels and what is stored. This page describes the code as it runs today.

Method version . Research only. Not investment advice. A verdict is about the record, not about the future.

Inputs

  • A file. A trade list or an equity curve as CSV or tab separated text, up to 5 MB and 50,000 rows. It is read and checked in your browser and never sent to our servers. Before the checks run you say whether it is real money, a demo account or a backtest (default: backtest), the starting balance, how many strategies, bots or settings you tried before this one (default: 1), and optionally what was claimed.
  • A description of a bot. A rule-based reader pulls out the claimed win rate, return, drawdown, trade count, take-profit, stop-loss and whether it adds to losers. Only the claim arithmetic and the settings count can be checked. A description cannot show an edge.
  • One of our tested strategies. Its equity curve from our research run, judged at the number of tries our research and search have counted.
  • Strategy code. TradingView Pine Script, a simple Python strategy, or rules in our rule format (including a description in words that your own AI model turned into rules). We run it ourselves on our daily prices; see strategy code.

From a record to returns

  • Trades and a starting balance become daily returns. Every weekday from the first close to the last is a day (a weekend day too when a trade closed on it), so quiet days count as zero. A day's return is its profit divided by the account value the evening before.
  • An equity curve becomes returns as (value minus money paid in that period) divided by the previous value, minus 1, so deposits are not profit. A return column is used as it is.
  • Trades with no starting balance have no returns. Card 3 then uses each trade's profit, and the largest fall from a high is not measured.
  • A series counts as daily when it has at least 180 values a year, weekly from 40, monthly from 10.

Strategy code

Code is read into one rule format, then run day by day on our daily closing prices. The same code always gives the same rules and the same result.

  • What is read. Pine Script versions 4 to 6: inputs, values worked out from the close (moving averages, RSI, MACD, Bollinger bands, highest and lowest, rate of change, standard deviation, crossovers), entries, closes, and exits with a stop, a target, or a loss or profit in ticks. Python: a signal or position column built with pandas (1 long, −1 short, 0 flat), vectorbt entries and exits, or a backtesting.py strategy class.
  • What is refused, by name and line. Anything the orders depend on that our data cannot support: the day's open, high, low or volume (so average true range, stochastics and trailing stops), other markets or time frames, values that carry over from day to day (:= or loops), orders that wait for a price, and partial closes. Reading a later price, such as a negative shift, is refused as peeking. Nothing is run until every refused part is gone. Drawings, alerts and values no order uses are listed as left out.
  • Fills. An order fills at the close of the day its rule is true. Pine fills at the next day's open, which for currencies and crypto, traded round the clock, is the same price. Stops and targets are checked against each day's close from the day after entry, since we hold one price a day; a real stop could fill earlier in the day, better or worse. One position at a time, using the whole account (or the share the script sets); an entry on the other side turns the position around.
  • Costs. Each fill pays the cost below, or the commission the script declares if that is higher. Overnight financing, borrowing to sell short and funding on perpetual futures are not charged.
MarketCost each wayWhy
  • Nearby settings. Every setting (inputs, and numbers written into an indicator) is also run at half to one and a half times its value: all combinations of 5 values for up to 3 settings, of 3 values for 4 settings, and one setting at a time beyond that. Lengths stay whole numbers.
  • Tries. The luck check counts the tries you state plus the nearby settings we ran, because finding settings like these usually means trying settings like those.
  • The result then goes through the five cards like any record, with the backtest's trades and its account value each day.

The verdict

The verdicts are checked in this order and the first that applies wins.

OrderVerdictWhen
1Not enough dataFewer than trades and fewer than daily returns
2Does not hold upA claim the file contradicts by more than 5 percentage points, or expectancy at or below zero after fees
3FragileA red flag on card 2: the worst loss wipes out more than average wins, a martingale or grid pattern, or a fall of more than from a high
4Could be luckThe luck check is below at the stated tries or cannot be measured, or any check on card 4 or card 5 failed
5Holds upNone of the above

The sentence under the verdict is built from the numbers that decided it, for example "Wins 99.0% of trades, but the 1.0% of losses are 150 times larger than the average win, so on average each trade loses 0.51."

A description is judged differently: "Does not hold up" when a claim fails on arithmetic, "Fragile" when the loss is not capped or there are too many settings for the trades, otherwise "Not enough data".

Card 1: win rate against expectancy

One result is a closed trade's profit after fees, or one period's return for an equity curve.

NumberDefinition
Win rateShare of results above zero
Average win, average lossMean of the winners, mean size of the losers
Payoff ratioAverage win divided by average loss
ExpectancyWin rate times average win, minus loss rate times average loss; also as a share of the starting balance when it is known
Break-even win rateAverage loss divided by (average win plus average loss). Equivalently 1 / (1 + payoff ratio)
Profit factorGross profit divided by gross loss

Card 2: tail risk

NumberDefinition
Worst tradeThe largest loss, in money and as a share of the account value before that trade (needs a balance)
Worst dayThe lowest daily (or period) return
Wins wiped outThe worst loss divided by the average win
Largest fall from a highMaximum drawdown of the account value, and the longest time below a previous high in calendar days
Expected shortfall (5%)Mean of the worst 5% of period returns (at least one)
SkewOf daily returns, or of trade profits when there are no returns

Martingale or grid. Needs position sizes on at least 90% of trades and at least 8 sized trades; otherwise the card says it cannot be checked. A losing streak counts when the record continues after it, and it "grew" when every trade after a loss in the streak is larger than the one before (by more than 0.01%). The pattern is found when there are at least 3 such streaks and size grew in at least 3 out of 4 of them, or when at least 3 times a new position opened on the same symbol and side at a worse price while an earlier one was still open.

Red flag when the worst loss wipes out more than average wins, a martingale or grid pattern is found, or the account fell more than from a high.

Card 3: the luck check

The luck check is the deflated Sharpe ratio of Bailey and López de Prado (2014): the probability that the true Sharpe ratio is above zero, once the number of things tried is allowed for. It is a line for line port of the function our research uses, tested against it to 3 decimal places.

DSR = Φ( (SR − SR0) × √(T − 1) / √(1 − skew × SR + (kurt − 1) / 4 × SR²) )
SR0 = √V × ( (1 − γ) × Φ⁻¹(1 − 1/N) + γ × Φ⁻¹(1 − 1/(N e)) ),  SR0 = 0 when N = 1
V   = (1 + SR²/2) / (T − 1)
  • SR is the mean over the sample standard deviation of the observations, not annualised. T is the number of observations, at least 30. Skew and kurtosis are the plain sample moments (kurtosis is 3 for a normal distribution). γ is the Euler constant, 0.5772. N is the number of tries.
  • V is a stand-in. The paper uses the spread of Sharpe ratios across all the tries. For an upload that spread is unknown, so V is the sampling variance of a single Sharpe estimate, as in our own research.
  • The line is , the same as test A3 of our own strategy tests.
  • "Survives up to N tries" is the largest whole number of tries for which the luck check is still or more, found by a search up to . 0 means it fails even as the only thing tried.
  • Minimum track record length (Bailey and López de Prado, 2012) is solved for the same check and stand-in: T* = 1 + ((z × D + √(1 + SR²/2) × K) / SR)², where z = Φ⁻¹(0.95), D is the denominator above and K the bracket in SR0. It is shown as months still needed at the record's own pace. It is never reached when SR is zero or below.
  • The observations are daily (weekly, monthly) returns when there are at least 30 of them, otherwise each trade's profit, which is how a trade list without a balance is judged.

Card 4: look-ahead and data flags

Every check is listed as passed, failed or "cannot be checked from this input". The last group is never hidden.

CheckFails when
Fills outside the day's rangeWhere we hold the day's high and low: an entry or exit price more than 0.1% outside them
Fills far from the day's priceWhere we hold one price a day: a fill further from that day's price than 3 times the 1-in-100 daily move of that market, and never less than 1%
Perfect timingWhere we hold the day's high and low, with at least 10 dated entries: more than 30% of entries within 0.05% of the day's best price (the low for buys, the high for sells). For strategy code: run again with every order filled one day after its signal, the Sharpe ratio falls below half of the original's, when the original is positive
Too smoothLag-one autocorrelation of returns above 0.3, or, for daily returns, no losing month in more than 24 months with under 3% losing days. Needs at least 30 returns. The measure follows Getmansky, Lo and Makarov (2004)
Edited historyAt least 10 trades: the record trades most days (median gap between trading days of 3 days or less) but has gaps of more than 30 days. For TradingView exports and plain trade lists, whose numbers run 1, 2, 3, also when at least 90% of the steps are 1 and some numbers are skipped. MetaTrader tickets are shared across a broker's server, so their skips are not checked
Backtest labelled as realYou said real money, but the file is a TradingView Strategy Tester export
Strategy look-ahead testFor our own strategies: the data is cut at several dates and no earlier position may change. For strategy code: each day's rules see that day's close and earlier ones only, and code that reads later prices is refused before it runs

The one-price-a-day limit. For major currency pairs, Bitcoin, Ether and the S&P 500 we hold one price a day from free sources, not the day's high and low. So for these the fill check becomes "fills far from the day's price", which catches made-up or misdated prices but not small ones, and perfect timing cannot be checked. A symbol we hold nothing for, or a record checked without our daily price file (for example signed out), gets "cannot be checked from this input". The only request Verify makes after a file is chosen is for that price file.

Card 5: overfitting flags

CheckFails when
Short sampleFewer than 100 trades, or less than 12 months (a month is 30.44 days)
First part against last partThe Sharpe ratio of the last 30% of the results is below half that of the first 70%, when the first is positive. Needs at least 30 results
ConcentrationAt least 10 trades and an overall profit: the best 5 trades make more than 50% of it
Many settings, few tradesDescriptions and strategy code: more than one adjustable setting per 20 trades
Settings next to yoursStrategy code: fewer than half of the nearby settings make money after costs

Claim arithmetic

  1. Break-even average loss = take-profit × win rate / (1 − win rate). A stated stop-loss wider than this means the average trade loses before costs if most losers hit it. With no stop, the card says nothing keeps losses under that figure.
  2. A stated largest fall smaller than a stated single loss is impossible.
  3. A monthly return above 10% kept for more than 12 months is shown as the yearly figure it compounds to, next to about 10% a year for the S&P 500 over the long run.
  4. A grid or martingale bot with no stated maximum loss is flagged "loss is not capped".
  5. A win rate above 90% with no stated average loss or stop gets "the win rate alone says nothing about profit".

Claims from a seller's page

A seller's page address or words can be checked on /verify/, with verify_claim and with POST /api/v1/verify/claim.

  • Reading the page. Our server opens a public page once, as ParityVerify/1.0, after reading the site's robots.txt (RFC 9309: our own group if named, otherwise "*"; the longest rule wins). It sends no cookies and no login, follows at most 3 redirects, each checked again, reads the first 2 MB and stops after 8 seconds. It never opens an IP address, a local name or a port other than 80 and 443. A page behind a login or a check for automatic visitors is reported, not worked round. X asks every automatic reader to stay out, so posts are pasted, not fetched. Nothing is kept.
  • Finding the claims. Fixed patterns read labelled values on statistics pages ("Growth: 212.48%", "Profit Trades: 481 (68.52%)") and claims in sentences ("92% win rate", "+340% in 12 months"): total, monthly and yearly return, win rate, largest fall, number of trades, trades a month, how long it ran, start date, profit factor, take-profit, stop, grid and martingale. Each claim keeps the words it came from. A label beats a sentence; of several drawdowns the largest is used, the reading kindest to the seller. Negations ("No martingale, no grid") are respected; forecasts, risk per trade, leverage and fees are not claims.
  • The person's own model, when they connect one, can add claims; each must quote words that are on the page, or it is left out.
  • Against a record. Win rate, monthly return, largest fall and total return use the "Claim against file" lines above and can change the verdict. Number of trades (within a tenth), profit factor, start date, length of time and yearly return are shown and never change it. Pips and a site's own Sharpe ratio are not compared.

The report

Every check ends in one report: the verdict in a sentence, the numbers that decided it, the claims, five pictures, the five checks, what was checked and what could not be. The pictures:

  • Growth: growth of 1 dollar (profit so far in money without a starting balance), next to the S&P 500's daily closes when our prices are loaded, which needs a signed-in account. Thinned to at most 240 points, keeping each stretch's lowest and highest.
  • Could it be luck, after every try: the luck check (card 3) at 1 to 1 billion tries, with the tries counted marked and the stretch above 0.95 shaded.
  • How sure is the result: with daily returns, the Sharpe ratio across 1,000 reshuffles in 21-day blocks; for a trade list, profit per trade across 1,000 draws of the trades with replacement (at least 200 for very long lists), seeded so it never changes. The 95% range is marked.
  • Falls from a high and where it wins and loses: calm and volatile markets and the named crises (with the S&P 500), each year, each market and long against short.

A report can be shared as a link. The report travels after the "#" of the address, which browsers never send, so Parity stores nothing and cannot vouch that a shared link was not changed; the page says so. Saving as PDF prints the same report with still pictures.

Batch screen for firms

The batch screen (/business/screen/ and POST /api/v1/verify/batch) judges many short records at once, such as the accounts in a prop firm's challenge. Method version . It uses the checks above, but its verdict is one of three, because a challenge lasts weeks and the "short sample" check on card 5 would fail every account.

OrderVerdictWhen
1Not enough tradesFewer than closed trades
2Likely luckExpectancy at or below zero after fees, a red flag on card 2 (worst loss over average wins, size growing after losses, or a fall of more than from a high), or the luck check below
3Holds upNone of the above
  • The luck check is card 3 on each trade's profit, at the account's own tries: the attempts column when the file has one, otherwise 1. One setting multiplies every account's tries by the number of accounts in the batch, the strictest reading of picking the best of a crowd. "Tries survived" is the largest number of tries at which the check stays at or above the line.
  • Passed comes from the file when it says. Otherwise an account passed when its balance, trade by trade, reached the profit target (default 8%) before the loss limit (default 10%).
  • Firm rules are reported, not used for the verdict: the best day making more than a set share of the profit (default 30%), fewer than a set number of trading days (default 5), size growing after losses, and the best 5 trades making more than of the profit. In a record of a few dozen trades the best 5 can make half the profit even with a real edge, so concentration is shown but is not a reason on its own.
  • The back-test uses an outcome per account (payout or blow-up). Among accounts with enough trades: the share of "likely luck" that later blew up, the share of blow-ups that were flagged, and the share of payouts that were flagged (false alarms), each with a 95% Wilson interval, and the gap between the blow-up rates of the two verdicts with Newcombe's interval.
  • Where it runs. On the page, files are read and checked in the browser and never sent. Through the API, a request is checked in memory and nothing is stored.

Imports

Verify reads exports from MetaTrader 4 and 5 (reports as saved, in HTML in any language or as the Excel "Open XML" file, and CSV), TradingView (CSV or the Excel download), Myfxbook, Interactive Brokers, Binance, Bybit, Coinbase, Kraken, cTrader, NinjaTrader, Tradovate, thinkorswim, Alpaca and any CSV with a close date and a profit.

  • Fills to trades. Where an export lists fills, they are paired first in, first out per symbol into round trips: each closing fill is one trade, so a partial close is its own trade, and its profit is the price difference times quantity and contract size, minus the fees of both the opening and closing fills (opening fees shared by quantity).
  • Fees as each platform means them. Interactive Brokers writes costs as negative and rebates as positive. A platform's own realized profit is used only for a close whose opening is before the file starts, as already net of fees (Interactive Brokers) or before them (Binance futures). Profit a platform already gives after commission (Myfxbook, NinjaTrader, cTrader's net) is not reduced again. A fee paid in a coin we cannot price, such as BNB, is named and left out.
  • Currencies. With a rate for each row (Interactive Brokers' rate to base currency), every amount becomes the account's base currency. A file in several currencies without rates says so.
  • Times. A time zone written in a cell or a header ("UTC", "Date(UTC+1)", "Closing Time (UTC-7)") is turned into UTC. MetaTrader writes the broker's server time and Interactive Brokers US Eastern time; days are counted in those times and the page says so.
  • Rows that cannot be read are named with their line and cell, for example "Line 3: the close date "2024-13-45" is not a date we can read."
  • Personal data. Columns that look personal (account number, name, email, server, login, IP address, and order ids that identify an account) are removed before anything else reads the file.

Each supported file is marked as tested on a real export, layout copied from a real export, or unverified layout (written from the platform's help pages). The full list is on the Verify page.

What Verify cannot check yet

  • Anything inside the day. We hold one closing price a day. Strategy code that needs the day's open, high, low or volume, intraday bars, trailing stops or orders that wait for a price is refused, and for an uploaded file "perfect timing" cannot be checked.
  • Other markets. Strategy code runs on 9 currency pairs, Bitcoin, Ether and the S&P 500 only. A stock, future or option strategy cannot be run yet.
  • Some costs. Overnight financing and swap, borrowing to sell short and funding on perpetual futures are not charged to strategy code.
  • Some code. Values carried from day to day (:=, var, loops), functions written in the script, other time frames (request.security), partial exits and adding to a position are refused by name and line.
  • Words. A description in words is only as good as the rules the person's own model writes from it. Verify shows those rules before running them; it cannot know what the person meant.
  • Some files. Bybit's spot trade history and its older closed P&L layout are written from help pages only; its closed positions export is copied from a real one. A fee paid in BNB cannot be priced. An Interactive Brokers activity statement in several currencies is not converted. Trades opened before a file starts have no known cost.
  • Where a file came from. An upload is self-reported: Verify checks the numbers in the file, not that a broker produced it. A broker-connected record shows where its trades came from, but not how many other things were tried (see below).

Trust levels

  • Self-reported. An uploaded file, strategy code, a description or a claim. Parity checked the numbers in the file, not where the file came from. The badge says so in those words.
  • Broker-connected. Parity's server read the trades from the broker or exchange itself, through a read-only link, ran the same checks on them as on a file, and signed the result. The badge names the venue, a one-way code for the account (the same code for every record from that account, never the account number) and when the trades were read.

What broker-connected shows

  • The trades came from that venue and one account, as the venue reported them when Parity read them, and nobody edited them before the checks ran.
  • Whether it is a live account or the broker's practice (paper) account, as the venue says. The badge says real money or paper account from that, not from the owner.
  • The numbers on the badge are the ones Parity worked out. The server signs them with an Ed25519 key, and a badge shows as broker-connected only when the signature checks out and every number on the badge matches the signed ones. Anyone can check this with the public key at /api/connect/keys.
  • The badge carries the SHA-256 of the trades file Parity read, which the owner can download and anyone can upload to Verify again.

What it does not show

  • How many other things were tried. The owner still states the tries, and the luck check depends on them. A broker shows one account; the owner may have other accounts, or other bots, that lost.
  • The starting balance, when the owner typed one. The badge says when it was stated.
  • Whose account it is, beyond the fact that the person who published it held a read-only key for it.
  • Anything outside the dates read, and costs the venue books apart from the fills, such as funding on contracts or fees paid in another coin. Each venue's gaps are listed on the Verify page.
  • That the venue's own records are right, or anything about the future. A verdict is never a recommendation.

How the link is kept read-only

A key is used for one read and then dropped. Only if the owner ticks "keep in sync" is it stored, sealed with AES-GCM under a key for that person which is itself sealed with a key only our server holds. It is used only when the owner asks for a new read, can be revoked in Settings at any time, and every read and use is logged for the owner. Reads are limited to 10 an hour and 40 a day per account.

Withdrawals stay visible. A withdrawn broker-connected badge loses its numbers and its proof, but its page still names the venue and the account code, so records from one account cannot be quietly removed.

What is stored

  • Never: the file, its trades, or the columns that look personal. If you leave the page, nothing remains on our side.
  • A badge, only if you publish one (signed in, up to 20 a day): the verdict, "survives up to N tries", the five cards' numbers, the kind of record, the stated tries, the date range, the trade count, the method version and the file's SHA-256, so anyone with the same file can check it. Withdrawing deletes the numbers and the page then says when it was withdrawn.
  • A broker-connected badge also stores Parity's signed proof: the venue, the account code, when the trades were read, the dates asked for, and fingerprints of the cards and of the trades file. Never the trades, the account number or the key.
  • Reading from a broker (signed in): a log row for each read, kept for the account owner, with the venue, the time and the outcome in words. A key is stored only for "keep in sync", sealed, until it is revoked.
  • The research dataset, only if you tick its box: summary numbers, the kind of record, asset classes and stated tries, with dates rounded to the month and no user id, file hash or name.
